# How X ranks posts and replies (as of 2026-11-20)

**Search budget note:** the session's web-search limit ran out near the end, so the Help Center pages (403 to automated fetches), posting time, Communities and Articles rest on fewer sources than the rest.

**Labels used below:** [P] = primary/official (X code, X staff or Musk posts, X docs). [M] = reported by several outlets. [S] = a single blog or study. [OLD] = dated before 2025, so possibly outdated.

## 1. The current system: Grok/Phoenix (2026)
- **Code release [P, M].** X published the For You code on 2026-01-20 at https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm (TechCrunch: https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-while-facing-a-transparency-fine-and-grok-controversies/). X says ranking "relies entirely" on a Grok-based transformer, Phoenix, with no hand-built relevance features.
  - Musk had said in Sept–Oct 2025 that the feed would be "purely AI" by November 2025 and that Grok would "read every post and watch every video" [M]: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-switching-to-fully-ai-powered-grok-algorithm/803174/
- **Pipeline [P, README].**
  - Thunder supplies posts from accounts you follow (in-network).
  - Phoenix retrieval, SimClusters and a popular-posts list supply posts from accounts you don't follow (out-of-network).
  - Phoenix ranking predicts the chance of each action, and the final score is the sum of weight × P(action).
  - The Grox classifiers (spam, adult, violent) run when a post is published.
  - Visibility filtering can allow a post, add a notice, put it behind an interstitial, or drop it.
- **Caveat.** Production values can differ from the repo (the README says so), and the Grox prompts are withheld "to reduce gaming."

## 3. For You vs Following
- **For You [P].**
  - Mixes in-network and out-of-network posts.
  - Posts from accounts you don't follow are multiplied by a factor below 1, and so are replies and reposts from accounts you do follow.
  - Each further post from the same author in a feed decays toward a floor.
  - Posts older than 48 hours are filtered out.
- **Following [P via Musk, M].** Since late November 2025, posts in Following are "ranked by Grok," with a chronological option still available. The app now opens on For You by default: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-sorts-following-feed-algorithm-ai-grok

## 4. New and small accounts
**Cold-start boost [P, code].** Source: `home-mixer/scorers/author_cold_start.rs`. Production values from param.rs:
- **Who qualifies:**
  - The author has at most 50,000 followers.
  - The post is under 2 hours old (7,200 seconds).
  - The post has fewer than 200 home impressions.
  - It is an original post, not a reply or repost.
- **What happens:**
  - About one such post per feed request is lifted to around position 15–16.
  - The pick uses Thompson sampling on the post's like rate against a prior of about 1.5%.
- **What it means:** the first two hours and the like rate on early impressions decide whether a small account's post gets the free slot.

**FairShareRankingFilter (2026-10-22) [S].** Reported by one outlet from a community reading of the code: https://shiftdelete.net/en/x-algorithm-update-provides-a-boost-to-small-accounts
- Before, fresh low-engagement posts were cut first when more than 2,800 posts were in line for scoring.
- Now cuts are made in proportion across sources.
- It is not in the README.

**NewUserMinEngagementFilter [P].** This applies to new viewers (accounts under 30 minutes old), not to authors.

**Musk's promise [M].** Musk said Grok ranking would help good small accounts get reach: https://www.thehansindia.com/technology/tech-news/elon-musk-says-grok-ai-to-scan-every-post-on-x-promising-better-reach-for-small-creators-1023037

## 5. Premium and verified
- **Reply priority [P].** X's Premium pages list "prioritized rankings in conversations and search," with the reply boost growing by tier (Basic < Premium < Premium+). I could not re-fetch help.x.com today (403), so this is long-standing official wording, not re-checked.
- **Feed code [P].** No Premium or verified parameter appears in the 2026 param.rs. The only subscription item is `IneligibleSubscriptionFilter`, which handles subscriber-only content.
  - The 2023 code had a boost for verified (Blue) authors, often cited as 4x in-network and 2x out-of-network [OLD].
- **Observed effect [S].** Buffer looked at 18.8M posts (Aug 2024–Aug 2025): Premium accounts got about 10x the reach per post, and Premium+ got the most. The study is observational, so better posters may simply pay more: https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/

## 6. External links
- **Bier, 2025-10-19 [P via X post, M].** X does not deliberately "deboost" links. Link posts reach fewer people because people engage less (the in-app browser covered the post). X tested a fix on iOS: https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/x-makes-overtures-to-journalists-with-new-feature-designed-to-improve-reach-for-links/
- **Code [P].** OpenLinkWeight is +0.2 and there is no link-penalty parameter. But link clicks push out dwell time and replies, which weigh more, and time spent now matters more.
- **Data [S].** Buffer (2025-10-14): non-Premium link posts had a median engagement of 0% since March 2025. Premium link posts still trailed text, image and video posts: https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/
- **Links in replies.** No official ranking rule says links in replies are treated differently. Putting the link in a reply is a creator habit. A claim that Musk said links have not been penalized for over a year came from one non-English outlet and is unverified.

## 7. Formats
- **Video [P].**
  - Video views carry zero weight in the score. What counts is dwell, time spent and continuation seconds.
  - A 10-second minimum duration exists, and there is an immersive-video scorer.
  - Video that holds attention wins; a raw view count does not.
- **Engagement rates by format [S] (Buffer 2025, regular / Premium):**

| Format | Regular | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| Text | ~0.40% | ~0.90% |
| Video | ~0.25% | ~0.85% |
| Image | ~0.20% | ~0.42% |

- **Long posts and Articles [P/M].**
  - Both are Premium features.
  - The October 2026 "read all the way through" retrieval favours posts people finish, not long posts as such [M].
  - Musk has pushed long-form since 2023 [OLD].
- **Communities.** I found no primary source on how Community posts rank, so treat any claim as unverified.

## 8. How replies are ranked under big posts
- **Signals, in order of how well they are documented:**
  - Premium tier (official).
  - Mutual follow: Bier's 2026-07-13 "small tweak" boosted mutuals' replies so reply sections stop feeling like a "battleground" with strangers [P via X post, M]: https://engadget.com/2214455/x-will-prioritize-replies-from-people-you-follow . It matches the 15.0 mutuals boost in the code.
  - Spam classifiers (Grox).
  - Low-quality replies get folded under "probable spam" or "additional replies" (long-standing UI behaviour).
- **Replies in For You.** `EnablePhoenixOonReplies` = false, so replies from accounts you don't follow are not served as standalone For You items.
- **Not public.** The ranker for reply threads under a post is not in the repo.

- **Posting time and frequency.** There is no primary source on time of day. The code-backed points are:

  - Spacing posts helps.
- **Community Notes.** A PNAS study from 2025 (on 2023 data) found posts lost about 46% of reposts and about 13.5% of views after a note was attached: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478135/ [S, peer-reviewed]

3. **"One report wipes out 468 likes."** The README rejects this explicitly.

5. **"Following is chronological."** It has been Grok-ranked by default since November 2025.
6. **"The algorithm gives verified accounts a fixed 4x boost."** That is the 2023 code; no Premium parameter exists in the 2026 code. Premium's documented advantage is reply and search priority.
7. **"Hashtags boost reach."** Musk says the system doesn't need them.
8. **"Small accounts get zero distribution."** The cold-start slot exists for authors with up to 50k followers.
9. **"Bookmarks are a top signal."** They are not a named action in 2026.

## Rules for an AI agent posting and replying on X for a small faceless account
1. **Win the first two hours.** Cold-start only lifts posts under 2 hours old with fewer than 200 impressions, picked by like rate. Post when the niche audience is online, and stay around to answer early replies.
2. **One strong post at a time, spaced out.** Back-to-back posts decay through author diversity. Keep the user's gap (90 minutes on X) and never post in bursts.
3. **Native video of at least 10 seconds, built to be watched to the end.** Views score nothing; time spent and finishing do. Put the payoff (the tool or result on screen) in the first 2 seconds, keep it tight, and caption it so it works muted.
4. **Write for replies, quotes and shares, not likes.** Replies, quotes and DM shares weigh 5.0, copy-link shares 20.0, likes 0.7. End on a concrete question or a useful "save or send this" hook. Don't use engagement bait that draws "not interested."
5. **No external link in the main post.** For a non-Premium account, links show near-zero engagement. If a link is needed, add it later as a reply or in the bio, and don't rely on that helping either.
6. **Avoid negative signals at all costs.** Not interested, mute, block and report are −31 to −234. Never spam, repeat text, tag strangers, or reply off-topic. Keep replies short and human (the hub's human-text rules), and cap 2 per thread.
7. **Reply by judgment, not by keyword.** X's automation rules forbid automated replies triggered only by keyword searches, and spammy or duplicate replies risk search removal or suspension (https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation; that page returned 403 when fetched today, so it was checked through search results only). Reply only where the agent adds something specific: fresh posts from mid-size accounts in the niche and people who engage with the account.
8. **Build mutuals and answer your own commenters.** Mutuals' replies get a 15x reply boost and rank higher in reply sections. Follow back relevant accounts and keep conversations going on your own posts.
9. **No hashtags (at most one, only if it truly fits) and no trend-jacking.** The ranker reads the content itself, and hashtags read as spam.
10. **Consider Premium and check visibility weekly.** Premium is the documented lever for reply ranking (with about 10x reach observed). Check "Under the Hood" or Account Status for visibility labels before warm-up sessions.

## Growing a faceless, AI-run X account fast (research as of 2026-11-20)

**The biggest risk comes first.** In July 2026 X removed 42,000 accounts that used chatbots to send replies. Nikita Bier, X's head of product, said that "using AI to programmatically engage with users without a human in the loop runs counter to our mission." X's automation rules also say that scripting the website (automation that doesn't use the API, which is what an agent driving Chrome does) "can lead to permanent suspension." They also require automated accounts to carry the "automated" label and be tied to an account a person runs. So AI-written replies at volume are the exact behavior X is hunting right now. Everything below assumes low reply volume, replies that sound like people, and ideally a person approving replies.

### How strong each source is
- **[X]** X's own code or statements.
- **[V-data]** Vendor study with a large dataset. Observational, not controlled.
- **[Anec]** Self-reported anecdote.
- **[3P]** Third-party claim with no method.

### What the algorithm actually is (2026)
- X open-sourced a ranker built on Grok on 2026-01-19 and updated it on 2026-05-15 [X] (https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm, https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-while-facing-a-transparency-fine-and-grok-controversies/). It predicts the chance of each action and sums them with weights.
- **Actions it scores up:** like, reply, repost, quote, share, profile click, video quality view, dwell time, and **following the author**.
- **Actions it scores down:** not interested, mute, block, report.
- **Diversity cut:** each extra post from the same author in one feed is scored lower.
- **Accounts you don't follow:** their posts get a multiplier below 1.
- **New authors:** there is a lift for authors with few impressions.
- **No published weights.** The widely quoted "reply 13.5x, author-answered reply 75x, Premium 4x/2x" figures come from the 2023 code. Treat them as [3P] for today's system.

### Tactics ranked by expected follower gain

**1. X Premium.** Safe. Evidence [V-data, strong for its type]
- Buffer studied 18.8M posts from 71k accounts (Aug 2024 to Aug 2025). Median reach was about 10x higher for Premium: over 600 impressions per post, against under 100 for free accounts. Free accounts' median engagement fell to roughly 0%. Sources: https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/ (Oct 2025), https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/ (2025-10-14).
- Caveat: accounts that pay are self-selected, so part of the gap isn't caused by Premium.
- X advertises reply prioritization for Premium. Articles and reply downvotes are also Premium-only.
- For a new account this is the biggest single lever. Without it, posts barely get seen.

**2. Original text posts of about 100-200 characters, plus image posts.** Safe. Evidence [V-data]
- Buffer: Premium text posts had about 0.90% engagement, video 0.85%, images 0.42%, links 0.28%.
- Ordinal (Jan 2024 to May 2026, B2B-weighted): text-only posts got about twice the impressions of image posts. Posts of 100-200 characters had the best engagement rate (https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-data-study).
- Buffer's 2026 report: text 3.56%, images 3.40%, video 2.96%, links 2.25% (https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement/, 2026-03-05).

**3. "Reply guy" (early replies on big niche accounts).** Safe at low volume. Grey at 30-60 a day. Gets restricted if it is automated mass replying. Evidence [Anec, weak]
- Sharbel ran 100 replies a day for 7 days: +350 followers on a 38.1k base, and he doubted replies were the cause (https://rosetta.to/u/sharbelxyz/i-replied-to-100-tweets-a-day-for-7-days).
- An Indie Hackers post claims 560k impressions in 10 days from zero followers, with about 80% of them from replies (https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-got-560k-impressions-on-x-in-10-days-with-zero-followers-and-zero-ad-spend-33c1e4d720).
- Changes working against it:
  - 2026-07-13: replies from mutuals are now ranked first, so strangers' replies sink (https://engadget.com/2214455/x-will-prioritize-replies-from-people-you-follow).
  - March 2026: Premium users got a reply downvote with an "AI generated" reason (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-adds-comment-downvotes-train-algorithm-tanking/815272/).
  - January 2026: reply impressions stopped counting toward creator payouts [3P].
  - Spam filtering hid 34 of 59 replies on one of Paul Graham's posts, and Bier said 95% of them came from LLM bots (https://techweez.com/2026/09/03/x-ai-generated-bot-replies/, 2026-09-03).
- Still the best way for a zero-follower account to be seen. It only works if replies are few, fast and human.

**4. Native video (screen-recorded tool demos).** Safe. Evidence [V-data, mixed]
- Ordinal: video gets high impressions but the lowest engagement rate. Buffer: video is near text for Premium.
- The "10-15x reach" claims are [3P] (timetopost.co).
- "TikTok-watermarked videos lose 90% of impressions" appears unsourced in Siècle Digital (2026-04-15). Avoid watermarks anyway.
- Fits the PC-tools niche well (before/after FPS or RAM demos).

**5. Threads.** Safe. Evidence [V-data plus X]
- Ordinal ranks threads highest on engagement rate. The exact value is missing from the page.
- Bier told publishers to put the substance in the post body or a thread instead of leaning on links (https://siecledigital.fr/2026/04/15/sur-x-lepoque-du-partage-de-liens-est-terminee-pour-etre-visible/).

**6. Quote posts.** Safe. Evidence: weak
- "Quote" is a scored action in the ranker [X]. No outcome data exists.
- Quoting a big niche post with a short take lands in your followers' feeds, not buried in the reply section.

**7. Profile (bio, pinned post, banner).** Safe. Evidence [3P]
- The ranker predicts profile clicks and follows [X].
- AutoTweet (a vendor) calls 2-5 profile visits per 1k impressions "average" and says a visit-to-follow rate under 2% means fix the bio or pinned post (https://www.autotweet.io/blog/twitter-analytics-explained-2026). There is no dataset behind those numbers.

**8. Articles (long posts).** Safe. Premium only.
- January 2026: Bier opened Articles to all Premium users and said some had gone viral. X ran a $1M prize (https://piunikaweb.com/2026/01/07/x-articles-premium-nikita-bier-updates/).
- No reach data. Good for a weekly "best free Windows tools" roundup.

**9. Posting frequency.** Evidence [V-data, correlational]
- Metricool: more weekly posts correlate with growth, from +0.12% at 1-2 a week to +0.66% at 10+ a week, with diminishing returns (https://metricool.com/twitter-study/).
- Candid's own test: going from about 3 to 10 posts a day raised total engagement, but engagement per post fell (https://blog.candid.org/?p=5821).
- The ranker's per-author diversity cut means extra posts close together compete with each other.

**10. Collaborations and shoutouts.** Safe if not paid and undisclosed. No data.

**11. Trending topics.** Safe only when relevant. Using automation to influence trends is banned. Off-topic hashtag hijacking counts as spam.

**12. Giveaways.** Grey.
- March 2026: Grok downranking of posts that "directly solicit engagement" was "very close" (https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/20/x-grok-downrank-engagement-bait-nikita-bier/).
- May 2026: a MrBeast giveaway was reportedly docked from payouts (https://piunikaweb.com/2026/05/02/x-deny-revenue-mrbeast-giveaway-engagement-bait/).

**13. Engagement groups / pods.** Gets restricted. X's manipulation rules cover aggressive or coordinated engagement. InfoFi "pay-to-engage" apps lost API access on 2026-01-15 (https://decrypt.co/354736/elon-musks-x-bans-access-infofi-crypto-projects-ai-slop-backlash).

**14. Follow/unfollow.** Gets restricted.
- Official cap: 400 follows a day (https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-follow-limit).
- A vendor test with 9,418 follows got a 3.0% follow-back rate, rising to 10.9% for targets with 10k-100k followers (https://dev.to/harvey_the_dev/x-twitter-follow-back-rate-in-2026-data-from-9418-strategic-follows-26c9, June 2026).
- X's developer policy names bulk-follow tools as manipulation.

**15. Bought followers.** Gets restricted. It is platform manipulation, and one 2026 review said 76% of delivered followers were gone within 90 days [3P] (hackernoon.com).

**Dead:**
- Communities shut down 2026-05-06, extended to 05-30 (https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/23/x-is-shutting-down-communities-because-of-low-usage-and-lots-of-spam/).
- Spaces still exist but don't suit a faceless AI account.

### What kills reach
- **Links in the post:** free accounts' link posts had 0% median engagement after March 2025 [V-data]. Put the link in a reply, or skip it.
- **Engagement bait** ("reply X", "RT to win"): Grok downranking [X statement].
- **AI-sounding or spammy replies:** hidden as "probable spam," downvoted, and possibly removed [X].
- **Repeated text:** duplicate filters [X], and duplicate content breaks the rules.
- **Mutes, blocks, "not interested," reports:** scored negatively [X]. Combative tone is reportedly penalized [3P].
- **Volume caps:** unverified accounts reportedly limited to 50 posts and 200 replies a day (NewsX, 2026-05-18, no X document cited).
- **Bought followers and follow churn.**

### Realistic numbers
**These are my estimates, not measured data.** No public medians exist. Vista Social reports that the median X profile barely moved and 42% of profiles shrank over three months.

| Day | Followers (Premium, 3-4 posts/day, 15-30 good replies/day) |
|---|---|
| 30 | 50-300 |
| 90 | 300-1,500 |
| 180 | 1,000-5,000 |

- Without Premium, divide by roughly 3-5.
- **Follows per 1,000 impressions:** about 0.2-1 typically. Replies that land under big posts convert better, around 1-3. Viral posts convert worse per impression.
- **Outliers:** one viral demo video or thread can add 1k-10k in a week. Anecdotes such as "30 to 1,000 in 14 days" exist but are cherry-picked [Anec].

### Mass growth playbook

**Before posting**
1. Buy Premium.
2. Set the "automated" label and link it to Krypt's own account, as X's rules require (https://help.x.com/en/using-x/automated-account-labels). Not doing this risks a total loss, which costs more than any reach the label might take away.
3. Bio: what the account gives in about 8 words, e.g. "free windows tools that actually work. fps, cleanup, ai."
4. Pinned post: the best demo video.
5. Banner: tool names and a screenshot.
6. Spend 3-5 days just reading, liking and following 30-50 niche accounts before the first post.

**Daily**
- **Posts:** 3-4 originals spaced 90+ minutes apart (the hub's gap):
  - 1 screen-recorded demo of under 30 seconds, no watermark.
  - 1 short text tip of 100-200 characters.
  - 1 image or meme.
  - Optionally 1 quote post.
- **No links in posts.** Put the link in your own first reply, only when asked.
- **Replies:** 15-30 a day, at most 2 per thread (the hub's rule), about 5 words, loose, lowercase okay. Never "Great point!" or other polished filler. Each reply should add a fact, number or joke.
- **Who to reply to:** a fixed list of 30-50 accounts with 10k-500k followers that post often:
  - Windows tweaking, gaming FPS and hardware creators.
  - AI-tool news accounts.
  - Polymarket and prediction-market accounts. Never claim profits; say "the AI trades for you" style only.
- **How fast:** within 5-20 minutes of their post. Scores decay quickly, and early replies get seen.
- **Answer every comment on your own posts within the first hour** (the author-reply signal).
- Ideally a person approves replies before they go out, given the July purge.

**Weekly**
- 1 thread or Article (a roundup or "how I set up X").
- 2-3 quote posts of the week's top niche post.
- Refresh the target list: drop accounts whose replies never convert, add rising ones.
- Check Account Status.

**What to measure (per post and per reply hour)**
- Impressions.
- Profile visits per 1,000 impressions.
- New follows per 1,000 impressions.
- Visit-to-follow %.
- Follows per 10 replies.
- Hidden or "probable spam" replies.
- Week-over-week change in median impressions.

**When to drop or double a format**
- Run each format at least 8-10 times over 2 weeks before judging it.
- **Double it** if follows per 1k reach at least 2x the account median.
- **Drop it** if it stays under 0.5x for 10 posts.
- If visit-to-follow is under 2%, rewrite the bio and pinned post before blaming the content.
- If replies get hidden, or median impressions drop more than 50% in a week: halve reply volume for 7 days, stop any repeated phrasing, and check Account Status.

### Evidence gaps
- No controlled studies exist for reply-guy growth, follower medians, or follows per 1k impressions.
- 2026 weight values are unpublished.
- The Premium reply-priority mechanics couldn't be confirmed because help.x.com blocked automated fetches. Pages from that site are cited via search results only.
- Bier's title is inconsistent in late-2026 coverage: one September article calls him "former" head of product.

## Turning X viewers into downloads and customers: research as of 2026-11-20

**How strong the evidence is.** No controlled study anywhere measures X-to-download conversion for software. The best data is Buffer's observational study of 18.8M posts (vendor, Oct 2025). Everything else is either an X executive's statement (official but unverified, and sometimes contradicted), a policy page (official), or a founder anecdote (self-reported). The rankings below are my judgement built on that evidence. They are not measured results.

### The biggest risk comes first
An AI agent that writes replies is exactly what X has been removing this year:
- **Feb 23, 2026:** X blocked API replies unless the original author mentions or quotes you ("Operation Kill the Bots"). https://piunikaweb.com/2026/02/24/x-api-blocks-automated-spam-replies/
- **May 28, 2026:** X began suspending accounts that use AI to automate replies while passing as human. The penalty was full suspension. https://piunikaweb.com/2026/05/28/x-suspend-accounts-ai-replies/
- **Jul 2026:** about 42k chatbot-reply accounts were reportedly removed. This comes from secondary sources only. https://startupfortune.com/x-removes-42000-ai-reply-bot-accounts-and-the-warning-to-growth-marketers-is-impossible-to-ignore/
- **X's developer guidelines (official, undated):** auto-replying to anyone who mentions a keyword is not allowed. Replies are allowed only if the user engaged first, at most 1 per interaction. Unsolicited auto-DMs are banned, including welcome DMs to new followers. Affiliate links in replies to random posts are banned. Bots should turn on the "Automated" profile label. https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines
- **Automation rules (help.x.com, updated Apr 2026):** "automated replies based on keyword searches" are not allowed. https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation

The browser-driven agent does not use the API, but the May suspensions targeted the behaviour, not the method. Our "human-sounding text" approach does not change what the policy covers. Whether to turn on the Automated label is a real trade-off for the user to decide.

### Tactics ranked by expected customers

**1. A native demo video with a before/after or a comparison, with the link outside the post body. Evidence: vendor, medium.**
In Buffer's data video beat links on every account type. Free accounts: links 0%, video ~0.25%, text ~0.40% engagement. Premium accounts: links ~0.28%, video ~0.85%. https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/ (Oct 14, 2025)

For the PC tools: show a 15-30s screen recording with an FPS counter, RAM or disk freed, or input lag, and say "free" on screen. A "this $30 tool vs our free one" comparison fits the versus style. Only use measured numbers you recorded yourself, never invented ones.

For the Polymarket bots: show the setup ("AI trades for you, easy"). Never show P&L or win rates.

**2. Hand-picked replies to people asking for a tool ("good free FPS booster?"). Evidence: anecdotal only, but buying intent is the highest.**
These probably convert best per impression, but this tactic is also the riskiest (see above).
- Keep it to a few genuinely relevant replies a day, never templated.
- Answer the question first. Add the link only if they asked for a tool.
- Never search for a keyword and reply to every match automatically.
- Founders report that replies drive most of their reach ("probably 80% of impressions"), but signup data is thin. https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-got-560k-impressions-on-x-in-10-days-with-zero-followers-and-zero-ad-spend-33c1e4d720

**3. Pinned post as a landing page, plus the bio and Website field. Evidence: none.**
I found no data on CTR for bios or pinned posts. The logic: every good reply or video sends profile visits, and the pinned post is what they see first. Make it a 30s demo, one line on what each tool does, and a tagged link in its first reply. Put a tagged krypt.cc URL in the Website field too. It costs almost nothing.

**4. Where to put the link. Evidence: contested, X's statements contradict the data.**
- **Nov 24, 2024:** Musk said to write the description in the main post and put the link in a reply.
- **Oct 2023:** Musk said "links don't get as much attention." https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2024/nov/26/elon-musk-hints-x-deprioritizes-links-keep-users-p/
- **Mar-Aug 2025:** Buffer found free-account link posts at a 0% median engagement rate.
- **Oct 19, 2025:** Bier announced an iOS test that keeps the post and its Like and Reply buttons visible while a link is open. He said links "tend to get lower reach." https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/x-makes-overtures-to-journalists-with-new-feature-designed-to-improve-reach-for-links/
- **Nov 3-4, 2025:** Substack's CEO said X traffic was "up substantially" even after removing preloads (self-reported). https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251105-x-post-url-link
- **Apr 2026:** Nieman Lab looked at 18 media accounts and found accounts that post many links get far less engagement. That is a correlation, not a test. https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260410-x-post-with-link-low-engagement-with-link
- **Jul 28-29, 2026:** Bier said "you do not need to put the links in replies anymore." Musk said "We haven't [penalised links] for over a year." PPC Land's reading of the open-sourced ranking model still finds ~94% fewer views on link posts, as learned behaviour rather than a coded penalty. https://ppc.land/x-drops-year-old-link-penalty-musk-tells-zuckerberg-on-platform/
- I found no confirmation that the in-app browser reached all users or Android.

**What to do:** for a small free account, put the link in the first reply or the pinned post by default. Then A/B test a link in the post body for 2 weeks with UTMs.

**5. Real-user testimonials and quote posts. Evidence: no X-specific data.**
Quote-post real users who praise a tool, and ask them for permission first. Never fabricate or AI-generate testimonials. The FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials has been in force since Oct 2024. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials

**6. Launch posts (new tool or version): one video, a changelog thread, the link in a reply. Evidence: anecdotal, spiky.** Good for bursts, not a steady channel.

**7. Build-in-public threads. Evidence: self-reported, weak.** Reported results include going from 4 to 100 waitlist signups in 30 days. https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-went-from-4-to-100-waitlist-signups-in-30-days-by-building-in-public-de3025f5f5 The fit is poor for a faceless account, because the audience is builders, not gamers or traders. Use it sparingly, as changelogs.

**8. Affiliate and creator programs. Evidence: none on X.**
- Paid posts need disclosure. X's built-in "Paid Partnership" label has existed since Mar 2, 2026. https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/x-ads-paid-partnership-labels-for-creators-so-they-can-ditch-the-hashtags/
- X changed its policy around Feb 19, 2026 to ban paid partnerships promoting gambling or "activities involving financial risk." Prediction markets are ambiguous: Kalshi pulled its affiliate badges, Polymarket kept its own. https://www.theblock.co/post/390983/kalshi-removes-x-affiliate-badges-after-policy-shift-tightens-promotion-rules
- So: paid promotion of the free PC tools is OK with the label. Do not pay creators to promote the Polymarket bots on X until that is clarified.

**9. X Articles. Evidence: none on reach.** Opened to all Premium subscribers on Jan 7, 2026. https://piunikaweb.com/2026/01/07/x-articles-premium-nikita-bier-updates/ Needs Premium. Low priority.

**10. Giveaways. Evidence: none; low-quality reach.**
X's rules: no rewards for duplicate posts, ban multiple-account entries, accept one entry per day. Contests can get entrants filtered out of search. https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-contest-rules They draw prize hunters, not users.

**11. DMs. Lowest value.** Free accounts can send 500 DMs/day (May 2026 limits). Unsolicited or automated DMs are banned by the developer guidelines. Only answer inbound DMs, at most one link. https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-limits

**12. Communities: gone.** X shut them down. They closed May 6, 2026, with migration extended to May 30. X said under 0.4% of users used them, while they produced 80% of spam and scam reports. Group chats in XChat replaced them. https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/23/x-is-shutting-down-communities-because-of-low-usage-and-lots-of-spam/

**X Ads:** not needed.

**Premium:** Buffer measured ~10x the reach of free accounts. https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/ It is the biggest single reach lever and the user's call. Free accounts since May 2026: 50 posts and 200 replies a day.

### Rules that apply
- **Spam / platform manipulation (help.x.com, Apr 2025 version):** bans bulk, duplicate or unsolicited posting, high-volume unsolicited replies and @mentions, and accounts where bare links are most of the activity. X may filter accounts that post many duplicate links out of search. https://help.x.com/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation So never post the same link text twice, and keep links a minority of posts.
- **AI disclosure:** X has a "Made with AI" label (Mar 2026). Undisclosed AI posing as human engagement is being enforced against.
- **Crypto and prediction markets:** the financial scam policy bans soliciting money by deception. https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/financial-scam Secondary reports say X auto-locks some accounts on their first crypto mention, so expect friction. https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/posting-crypto-on-x-for-the-first-time-you-might-hit-the-kill-switch/
  - Never make profit, win-rate or "passive income" claims.
  - Never use the words "free to use" or "open source" for the bots.
  - Add a plain risk line ("trading involves risk").
  - X has called Polymarket its official prediction-market partner since Jun 6, 2025, but that is no endorsement of third-party bots. https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/06/x-names-polymarket-as-its-official-prediction-market-partner/
- **Disclosure of paid posts:** use the Paid Partnership label or #ad. The FTC Endorsement Guides apply. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

### Measuring
- **UTMs:** use a distinct one per placement, e.g. `?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<tool>&utm_content=<reply|pinned|bio|post-<id>>`. On krypt.cc count downloads per UTM, not just visits.
- **In-app browser preloads inflate clicks.** Substack saw this in Nov 2025, so judge by downloads.
- **X analytics:** free accounts get per-post stats: impressions, engagements, profile visits, and possibly link clicks (Buffer says yes, Hootsuite omits it). The full account dashboard is Premium-only. https://blog.hootsuite.com/twitter-analytics-guide/
- **Weekly funnel to track:** impressions → profile visits → link clicks (by UTM) → downloads → active installs.

## Playbook: pushing the product

**Funnel.** Demo video or comparison post → link in the first reply → profile → pinned demo with a link → tagged krypt.cc page → download. Hand-picked replies to tool requests feed the profile.

**Daily (free account, well under the 50/200 limits):**
- 1-2 native video posts, each a single tool's before/after or a "vs paid tool" comparison. Text in the post, link only in the first reply. Rotate tools; never repeat link text.
- 3-6 replies to people who explicitly ask for a tool or have a matching problem. They should read as unique and useful. A link only when it answers the ask, and at most 2 per thread.
- Answer everyone who replies to our own posts. No outbound DMs.
- Polymarket bots: at most 1 post a day, setup and ease only, with a risk line and no numbers.

**Weekly:**
- One launch or changelog post.
- One quote post of a real user, with permission.
- One test: link in the post body vs in a reply.
- Refresh the pinned post with the week's best demo.
- Check Account Status.

**Measure weekly:** downloads per UTM placement, link clicks per 1k impressions, and profile visits per post. Double down on the formats that bring downloads, not the ones that bring likes.

**Main risks for an AI agent:**
1. Suspension for undisclosed automated replies (actively enforced in 2026). Keep replies low-volume and hand-picked, consider the Automated label, and never reply to keyword matches automatically.
2. Duplicate or link-heavy activity gets filtered out of search.
3. Profit or "free" claims on the bots, plus crypto auto-locks.
4. Fake or AI-made testimonials (FTC).
5. Paid creator posts about the bots under X's Feb 2026 gambling and financial-risk partnership ban.
6. Misreading inflated click counts.

**Where data is missing:** pinned-post and bio CTR, whether link posts still lose reach (X says no, the outside data says yes), conversion rates from replies, reach of Articles, and Android in-app browser status. Our own UTM data will be the only real evidence.

## X (Twitter) for an AI agent in a real Chrome browser: limits, warm-up and risks (as of 2026-11-20)

Tags: **[O]** official (X Help Center, X Rules, docs.x.com) · **[M]** several independent sources · **[A]** anecdotal or one vendor. help.x.com blocks automated fetches, so I read the Help Center pages from Wayback 2026 snapshots. Searching hit the session budget near the end, so the "new-account reply/country-label" angle was not searched separately.

### The main finding: X's rules ban browser automation itself
- **[O]** Automation rules (updated April 2026), "Don't!" list: *"Use non-API-based forms of automation, such as scripting the X website. The use of these techniques may result in the permanent suspension of your account."* https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation
- **[O]** Developer Guidelines: *"Non-API automation (scraping, browser automation) results in permanent suspension."* They also require the "Automated" profile label, a bio note saying it is a bot and who runs it, and a link to a human-run account. https://docs.x.com/developer-guidelines
- **[M]** On 2026-02-14, X head of product Nikita Bier said: "If a human is not tapping on the screen, the account and all associated accounts will likely be suspended — even if you're just experimenting." https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/x-has-introduced-new-spam-automation-detection-measures/story
- **[M]** Around 2026-05-28, Bier suspended accounts that ran autonomous AI reply agents posing as people. Using AI to proofread is still fine. https://piunikaweb.com/2026/05/28/x-suspend-accounts-ai-replies/
- **[O]** The Help Center says AI reply bots need *"prior written and explicit approval from X."* The developer docs say "no written approval needed." The two pages conflict.

So no warm-up makes an agent in a browser compliant. Pacing only lowers the chance of being caught. "Associated accounts" matters for Marketo: every install follows, visits and reposts Krypt's account. The policy calls that "coordinating to exchange engagement" and "engaging… through automation to drive traffic." So Krypt's own account is at risk too. **[O]** https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation

### Official limits [O] (Help Center "About X limits", 2026 snapshot)
- **Posts:** "50 original posts and 200 replies per day for **unverified** accounts." This came in around 2026-05-18. The old 2,400 figure is still on the page but now looks out of date. Daily limits are split into semi-hourly windows; the per-window numbers are not published. https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-limits · https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/x-sets-daily-caps-for-free-unverified-accounts-50-posts-200-replies-and-500-dms-enn26051903410 (2026-05-19) [M]
- **DMs:** 500 a day. **Follows:** 400 a day, "and there are additional rules prohibiting aggressive following." Past **5,000** followed, account-specific ratios apply. Email changes: 4 an hour.
- The limits count actions from every device and the API. X can lower them when the site is busy.
- **Read/view limits:** the 2023 ones (6,000/600/300 posts) are no longer on the limits page, and no 2026 read cap is published.
- **Phone:** there is no published phone rule just for new accounts. The anti-spam challenge is a lock that asks for a phone number or a puzzle. [O]
- **API changes:**
  - Since 2026-02-23, replies through the API are allowed only when the author mentions or quotes you first.
  - Since 2026-04-20, follows, likes and quote-posts are removed from all self-serve API tiers.
  - An original post costs $0.015, or $0.20 with a link. [O] https://docs.x.com/updates/changelog

### What the automation rules ban [O]
- **Likes:** "You may not like posts… in an automated manner." No exceptions.
- **Replies and mentions:** automated replies to users who did not ask for them are not allowed, and neither are replies chosen by "keyword searches alone." An automated reply is allowed only after the user opts in, with a way to opt out, and only one per interaction.
- **DMs:** no unsolicited automated DMs. Someone following you is not consent.
- **Follows:** no bulk, aggressive or indiscriminate following or unfollowing. "Follow churn" breaks the rules.
- **Reposts:** allowed for information or entertainment, but not bulk or aggressive.
- **Content:** no duplicate or near-identical posts ("copypasta"), no automated posting on trending topics, no misleading or redirecting links.
- **Off-topic replies:** "Promoting content by replying with content that is irrelevant" counts as spam.

### What gets new accounts restricted, and how to check
**Enforcement steps [O], from the policy's "How we enforce" section:**
- Anti-spam lock asking for a phone number or a puzzle (the puzzle is reported to be Arkose).
- Posts removed from search, trends and For You.
- Replies pushed down ("downranking the post in replies").
- Features such as DMs or posting switched off for a while.
- Permanent suspension "at first detection" for severe cases.

**Triggers:**
- **[O]** Bulk or identical replies, @mention spam, links posted without comment, follow churn, aggressive liking or reposting.
- **[M]** A March 2026 wave of bans cited "aggressive and random repost and/or liking" and "indiscriminate following." It was partly a filter bug, and 99% of those accounts were restored. https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/12/x-users-report-wave-of-bans-for-inauthentic-behaviors/
- **[A]** A vendor (OpenTweet, updated 2026-08-31) reports about 42,000 accounts removed on 2026-04-09 for chatbot replies. It names these signals: datacenter or VPS IPs, many reposts compared with original posts, no normal browsing, and accounts moving in lockstep. https://opentweet.io/answers/did-x-suspend-my-bot-in-the-purge

**Checks:**
- **[M]** Settings > Your account > Account status. It shows only labelled restrictions, so a clean page proves little.
- **[M]** Logged out, search `from:handle` (search ban) and type the @handle into the search box (suggestion ban).
- **[M]** Open one of your replies in a big thread from another account and see whether it sits under "Show more replies" (reply deboost).
- **[A]** Third-party checkers: Shadowban Radar, Sorsa, Circleboom, BanScope and others. A roundup rechecked them on 2026-11-05: 27 work, and shadowban.eu is dead. None has been tested independently, and new accounts often fail them falsely. https://shadowbanradar.com/best-x-shadowban-checkers
- **[A]** "Under the Hood" is said to show last month's labels, for accounts at least a year old. Only one source mentions it.

### Does a warm-up help?
X has never said so officially. Vendor guides and forums all say yes [M/A]: browse first, ramp up slowly, keep a steady human pace, and use a home or mobile IP rather than a datacenter one. https://www.bitbrowser.net/blog/how-to-warm-up-x-twitter-accounts-safely-2026 · https://dev.to/ipfoxy/2026-how-to-avoid-x-twitter-shadowbans-account-bans-124g

**7-day plan for a brand-new account [A]** (sessions 15–30 min, at varied times):

| Day | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Profile, photo and bio. Confirm email and phone. Read only. Follow 3–5 relevant big accounts. |
| 2 | Read. 5–10 likes, spread out. |
| 3 | Read. 10 likes, 5 follows. |
| 4 | Same as day 3, plus 1–2 real replies to big accounts in your niche. No links. |
| 5 | First original post: a native video, no link. 3 replies, 10–15 likes. |
| 6 | 1 post, 3–5 replies, 1 repost, 5–8 follows. |
| 7 | 1–2 posts. Reply to everyone who replied to you. Check Account status. |

No DMs and no links before week 3.

**An account that is already active:** raise volumes about 20–30% a week from what it really did over the last 30 days, never jumping straight to the target. After any lock or challenge, cut to half for 7 days.

### Safe daily volumes (unofficial) [A]
These are compiled from vendor and forum ranges: 20–30 actions a day in weeks 1–2, and 50–100 follows a day only after 3–4 weeks. They reduce detection risk; they do not make automation compliant.

| Account age | Posts | Replies | Likes | Reposts | Follows | DMs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 0–2 | 0–5 | 5–15 | 0–2 | 3–8 | 0 |
| Weeks 2–4 | 1–3 | 5–10 | 15–30 | 1–3 | 5–15 | 0–3 (replies to people who wrote first) |
| Months 2–3 | 2–5 | 10–20 | 30–60 | 2–5 | 10–30 | ≤5 |
| 3+ months, healthy | 3–8 | 15–30 | 50–100 | 3–8 | 20–50 | ≤10 |

On the user's current settings (older account: 20 posts, 20 replies, 90 scrolling minutes):
- 90 minutes of scrolling is fine. It is exactly the "organic browsing" signal detectors look for.
- 20 replies is within the range for an established account.
- 20 posts is a soft-cap risk. It is under the official cap of 50, but it is high for a human-looking account. With the 90-minute post gap, only about 16 posts fit in a day anyway. 5–8 is more realistic.

### Premium vs free
- **[O]** The 50-post / 200-reply cap applies to unverified accounts only; Premium lifts it. The exact Premium numbers are not published. https://engadget.com/2175771/x-free-accounts-limited-to-50-posts-and-200-replies-a-day [M]
- **[O]** Premium gets "reply prioritization": larger on Premium, largest on Premium+. So free accounts' replies are pushed down by design, not only through penalties. https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-premium
- **[O]** Media upload size and length limits follow the account's Premium status (changelog, 2026-09-01). Free accounts have been limited to about 2:20 per video (my own knowledge, not re-checked).
- **[M/A]** Premium is widely reported to lower the chance of spam flags, because a paid, phone-verified identity counts for something. It does not exempt anyone from the automation rules, and paid accounts were suspended in the 2026 waves.

### Hard rules for an AI agent running X in a browser
1. Treat the whole setup as against X's rules (scripting the website). Tell users plainly that permanent suspension is possible, including of "associated accounts".
2. **Never like automatically.** It is banned without exception. Likes should be a human click, or skipped.
3. Reply only to posts in the account's own niche, and make each reply unique and on topic. Never pick targets by keyword alone. No links, and no @mentions of people who never engaged.
4. No DMs unless the other person wrote first; never a welcome DM. Stay at or under 2 messages a thread, which the hub already enforces.
5. No follow/unfollow churn. Follow only relevant accounts, a handful an hour, and never unfollow in bulk.
6. Never post the same or near-same text or video, on one account or across installs. Across installs this is the biggest risk for the Krypt follow/visit/repost scheme.
7. Browse and read throughout every session, with irregular gaps. Never use datacenter or VPS IPs; one sticky home or mobile IP per account.
8. Stop at the first lock, challenge, "temporarily limited" banner or error. Hand it to the human. Never solve captchas or retry automatically, and back off 24–72 hours.
9. Check Account status and a logged-out `from:` search every day, and halve volume if anything looks wrong.
10. Lower-risk path: post original videos through the official API (allowed, about $0.015 a post), and make replies, likes and follows require one click from the user. For X, that means approvals on by default.

### Patterns that repeat across the winners

1. **A short hook plus media, at high volume.** The news-style brands post 25-75 times a day. Their engagement rate is low (0.4-0.5%), but they win on reach.
2. **"BREAKING / NEW" framing** carries the prediction-market accounts. It is also what got them scrutinized.
3. **Free-resource hooks** ("X is offering free…", "this saves you money") get the most bookmarks, and bookmarks are what lists of tools earn.
4. **Concrete surprise in the first line.** One specific fact, no preamble.
5. **Links stay out of the post.** They go in the bio (link page or storefront) or in "link in comments", or come through comment-a-keyword-then-DM.
6. **Demo video of the tool working** is used for AI and app tools.
7. **Off-niche viral posts build the audience**, and the bio turns it into customers (levelsio, Pirat_Nation).
8. **Prediction-market brands use a meme/troll voice** (Kalshi).
9. **Big prediction-market reach was bought, not built:** purchased accounts and affiliate networks. You can't copy that, and it is now a risk.
10. **Replies are a small share of engagement.** Originals drive growth, and replies are for being seen.

### What has stalled or been banned

- **AI reply bots:**
  - Feb 2026: X cut automated API replies unless the author mentions or quotes the bot.
  - May 2026: X suspended accounts for automated AI replies (@heygurisingh and three others). AI for proofreading is still allowed.
  - Jul 24 2026: X removed 42,000 chatbot reply accounts.
  - Aug 2026: X hid 34 of 59 replies under a Paul Graham post. Bier said 95% were from language models. The signal reported was "polished, agreeable comments that say very little." Real users get caught too.
- **Prediction-market promotion:**
  - Feb 18 2026: X's Paid Partnerships policy banned paid gambling promotion.
  - Feb 23 2026: Kalshi pulled all its X affiliate badges.
  - Mar 2026: CJR showed Polymarket-affiliated "news" accounts spreading false BREAKING posts.
  - Jun 2026: the WSJ reported Polymarket paid creators to show simulated trades with fake wins on clones of its site. Two senators then asked the CFTC to review Polymarket's marketing.
- **Fake P&L and scam bots:**
  - "$10K → $500K" bot screen recordings are being called out as demo or edited accounts.
  - A study reported by Cybernews found every AI model it tested lost money on Polymarket and Kalshi.
  - Key-stealing Polymarket bots: the Trust412 copy-trade bot (Dec 2025), the hijacked dev-protocol GitHub org with 20+ repos and inflated stars (Feb 2026), and 30 fake npm packages (Jul 2026).
