## YouTube Shorts ranking and distribution, as of 2026-11-20

**How each point is tagged:** [OFFICIAL] means YouTube Help, YouTube Blog, Creator Insider or YouTube staff. [MULTI] means reported by several outlets. [SINGLE] means one blog or analyst. **PRE-2025** means it may be outdated.

### 1. Ranking signals

- **YouTube publishes no weights or thresholds.** In 2023, Shorts product lead Todd Sherman said the view threshold is kept private so people can't game it, and that the advice is "think audience, not algorithm." [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**, Aug 2023] https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/
- **General recommendation signals:** YouTube's own page names viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions, satisfaction surveys and channel reputation/quality. It does not list Shorts-specific signals, and it does not name watch time or shares. [OFFICIAL] https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/
- **How the Shorts feed differs:** viewers swipe without choosing what comes next, so the feed favours more varied content than long-form does. [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**, 2023]
- **"Viewed vs swiped away":** Sherman called it a great metric to watch in 2023. YouTube has never said it is the ranking signal. [MULTI, **PRE-2025**]
  - The current Help Center text describes a Shorts metric called **"Stayed to watch"**: "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short."
  - It also defines **engaged views** ("stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops"). Average view duration and average percentage viewed are both calculated from engaged views.
  - So loops are left out of engaged views and retention numbers. Whether loops help ranking is not stated anywhere official.
  - Check the exact label in Studio; the Help Center no longer uses the words "swiped away." [OFFICIAL] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281
- **Likes, shares, comments, subscribes from a Short:** no official weighting exists. The 2026 blog figures (for example "VVSA = 65% of score") are invented. [SINGLE, ignore]
- **Early read on performance:** creator Jordan Howlett, on YouTube's own blog, judges a Short within about an hour by its like-to-view ratio. That is a creator's habit, not an official signal. [OFFICIAL blog, creator opinion, Aug 4 2025] https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/five-tips-to-master-shorts/

### 2. Distribution, and why Shorts take off late

- **Seed audience:** the system shows a Short to a small group likely to enjoy it. It "may get a lot more traffic or it may taper off," which is why a Short can stall and then pick up later. [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**, Sherman 2023] https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/
  - Fixed "waves" and named stages are blog inventions; YouTube has never described them.
- **Do not delete and re-upload a Short to restart it.** Sherman: "I would not advise that"; it can be seen "as spam in our systems." [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**] https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-shares-advice-for-shorts-creators-including-notes-on-hashtags-the/691835/
- **Freshness theory:** analysts (Mario Joos, Tim Chesney) say Shorts older than about 28–30 days lost impressions from mid-September 2025. Joos himself calls it a "working theory"; YouTube has not confirmed it. [MULTI analysts, unconfirmed, SEJ Dec 5 2025] https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm-may-now-favor-fresh-over-evergreen/562661/
- **Small channels:** the Shorts feed is largely detached from subscriber count.
  - Metricool's 2026 study (about 800k videos) found channels under 10k subscribers had the strongest gains. [SINGLE study, Aug 2026] https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/
  - YouTube has published nothing official that is specific to small channels.
- **Length:**
  - Square or vertical videos up to 3:00 uploaded on or after Oct 15 2024 are classified as Shorts.
  - **Any Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide:** it can't be played, recommended or monetized.
  - In 2023, Sherman said no length is favoured: make it as long as the story needs (**PRE-2025**).
  - YouTube has not said that shorter Shorts rank better. The "15–30s is best" figure comes from blogs. [OFFICIAL] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877 ; https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/ (Oct 2024)

### 3. Originality, reused and inauthentic content, AI

- **Jul 15 2025:** the "repetitious content" policy was renamed **"inauthentic content"** and now covers mass-produced and repetitive videos. [OFFICIAL] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392
  - Not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production," image slideshows, and templated storylines with little narrative or educational value.
  - Allowed: the same intro and outro on every video "but the bulk of your content is different."
  - The reused-content policy was not changed. Clips "edited together with little or no narrative" are not monetizable. It applies to the whole channel, and having the original creator's permission does not exempt you.
- **Jul 16 2026 clarification (YPP, so monetization only):** three categories are now named. [MULTI: TechCrunch Jul 20 2026, Tubefilter Jul 13 2026, quoting YouTube's Matt Halprin]
  1. Generic, repetitive or template-made content. This includes tutorials that repeat what is already common on YouTube.
  2. "Unsatisfying or off-putting" content: manipulative formulas, interchangeable formats, unrelated AI clips stitched together.
  3. **AI personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics.**
  - https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/
- **Oct 1 2026, Creator Insider (Rene Ritchie):** the Shorts recommender now **favours original Shorts and cuts reach** for channels that mainly re-upload other creators' clips. This is reach, separate from monetization. [OFFICIAL + MULTI]
  - Not enough to count as original: "VO descriptions of what's happening on screen, minor technical edits, or template-based bulk changes."
  - What counts: your own commentary, analysis or storytelling.
  - "The recommendation system will re-evaluate reach" once a channel changes course.
  - Clipping your own long-form is reportedly unaffected.
  - Twitch/Kick clipping channels fall squarely in scope. Credit in the caption is not mentioned as a fix.
  - Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XS2A6aExLaA ; coverage: https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/ , https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/
- **AI disclosure** (Studio upload: Details > Attributes > "AI use": Yes/No). [OFFICIAL] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
  - Required for realistic, meaningfully altered or synthetic content: a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered real events, realistic scenes that never happened, AI music as the focus.
  - Not required for: AI-assisted scripts, titles, thumbnails, captions, upscaling, cloning your own voice, game footage, or non-realistic content.
  - Generic text-to-speech narration over screen recordings is not addressed. It most likely needs no disclosure if it doesn't imitate a real person.
  - If you repeatedly fail to disclose, YouTube may add the label itself or remove content or suspend you from YPP.
  - From **May 2026** YouTube labels detected photorealistic AI automatically. On Shorts the label is an overlay on the video.
  - A label "doesn't change how a video is recommended" or whether it can earn money. https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-for-viewers-and-creators/ (May 27 2026)
- **Gambling:** since March 2025, sending viewers to unapproved gambling sites is banned, and videos that depict or promote online gambling are age-restricted. [MULTI]
  - Prediction markets are in a regulatory grey zone. Google's Oct 30 2026 ad rules put them in a sensitive category. [MULTI] https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/05/youtube-cracking-down-on-gambling-online-casinos/ ; https://cdcgaming.com/brief/youtube-expands-personalized-gambling-ads-but-prediction-markets-face-limits/
- **YPP thresholds from Feb 1 2027:**
  - New entrants need 8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - To share Shorts ad revenue at all, a channel will need 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - [OFFICIAL, Aug 10 2026] https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/

### 4. Titles, descriptions, hashtags, search, links, remix

- **Hashtags:** up to 3 of the most engaging show above the title. **More than 60 hashtags and all of them are ignored.** Over-tagging or irrelevant hashtags can get a video removed from search or uploads. [OFFICIAL] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658
  - The common "15 hashtag limit" is a blog myth [MULTI blogs, wrong].
- **#shorts is not needed:** a video is classified as a Short by aspect ratio and length alone (≤3:00, square or vertical). [OFFICIAL by implication, 15424877]
- **Hashtags are optional:** Sherman said they help for events and topics. [OFFICIAL, **PRE-2025**]
- **Related video link:** you can attach one link per Short to a video on your own channel, in Studio > Details.
  - YouTube's July 2026 tips: put a call to action pointing at the link, and make the first seconds of the long video deliver what the Short promised.
  - Other official tips in the same piece: remix (audio, green screen, cut), templates, trending audio from Add Sound, browsing hashtag pages for ideas, poll/quiz/Q&A stickers, and "Add Yours" stickers for reach.
  - [OFFICIAL, blog summarised by Social Media Today, Jul 19 2026] https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/grow-youtube-channel-interactive-shorts/ ; https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-offers-tips-on-working-with-shorts-videos/
- **Trending sounds:** they "can increase discoverability" but are "not essential." [OFFICIAL, May 6 2025] https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/
- **Search:** YouTube ranks search results by relevance, engagement and quality. There is nothing official on Shorts-specific search. [OFFICIAL, howyoutubeworks]

### 5. Posting frequency and timing

- **Frequency:** posting more Shorts neither expands nor reduces reach in itself, but many low-quality Shorts can hurt. Sherman: that "energy is probably better spent in just making a better video and fewer of them." [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**]
- **Timing:** YouTube does not optimise Shorts for time of day, except for news. [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**]
  - vidIQ's data (about 40M videos): upload hour barely matters; weekends are modestly better. [SINGLE]
- **No official cadence number exists.**

### 6. Shorts and long-form

- **Official position:** Shorts and long-form use separate recommendation systems, and "we haven't seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance." [OFFICIAL, May 6 2025, myths post above]
- **2023 Creator Insider:** channels making both formats grew faster. [OFFICIAL via MULTI, **PRE-2025**]
- **Shorts views don't feed long-form recommendations:** vidIQ reports Shorts watch history has not informed long-form recommendations since 2022. [SINGLE, **PRE-2025**]
  - That is why Shorts subscribers often don't watch long-form. The related-video link is YouTube's official bridge between the two.
- **Analyst reports, no official confirmation:**
  - Home feeds show fewer long-form videos (Dec 2025, Mario Joos). [SINGLE]
  - Long-form views +76% but engagement −45% year on year (Metricool 2026). [SINGLE]
- **View-count changes:**
  - **Mar 31 2025:** a Short's view counts when it starts or replays; the old definition was renamed "engaged views." [OFFICIAL/MULTI]
  - **Aug 24 2026:** first-frame counting extended to all formats.
  - Earnings, analytics (AVD, retention) and recommendations still use **engaged views**; "no change to recommendations." [OFFICIAL, Aug 19 2026] https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/
  - Raw view counts are inflated and not comparable across those two dates.

### 7. Myths that official sources contradict

- **"#shorts is required":** classification is by format, not by the tag.
- **"Shorts kill your long-form":** YouTube says it has seen no evidence of that (2025).
- **"More uploads = more reach":** Sherman said no; quality over quantity.
- **"Delete and re-upload to get a second chance":** this risks being treated as spam.
- **"Post at the magic hour":** time of day is not a factor YouTube optimises for Shorts.
- **"An AI label kills reach":** YouTube says the label has no effect on recommendation or monetization.
- **"You need trending audio":** helpful but not essential.
- **"Exactly 15 hashtags max":** the official cutoff is 60.
- **"Rising view counts after 2025 mean the algorithm got better":** only the counting method changed.

### Rules for an AI agent posting Shorts for a small faceless channel

1. **Every Short must carry the channel's own value.** Use real screen recordings of the tools in use, with a specific claim, comparison or result. Narration that just describes what's on screen over borrowed footage is explicitly not "original" (Oct 2026). Don't post Twitch/Kick or other creators' clips unless the Short is mostly your own commentary or analysis; credit in the caption is not enough.
2. **No template farming.** Vary the hook, structure, script and visuals between uploads. Same intro and outro is fine; same narration over swapped clips is "inauthentic content," and that penalty applies channel-wide.
3. **Don't present the trading-bot narrator as a human financial expert.** AI personas on financial topics are now a named non-monetizable category.
   - Make no profit promises.
   - Don't push viewers toward unapproved gambling or betting sites.
   - Treat Polymarket content as sensitive: plain "here's how the tool works" demos, no "guaranteed returns."
4. **Fill in the "AI use" attribute honestly in Studio.** Answer Yes for any photorealistic synthetic footage or a voice that imitates a real person. Generic text-to-speech over real screen captures, AI scripts and AI captions generally need No. Never fake a real person's voice.
5. **Win the first seconds.** "Stayed to watch" and engaged views measure exactly that. Open on the result (the before/after, the tool doing its thing), not on a logo or "hey guys." Keep the length to what the demo needs; nothing official favours 15s over 60s.
6. **Avoid copyrighted music on Shorts over 60s.** Any active Content ID claim blocks a 1–3 minute Short worldwide. Use the Shorts audio library or no music on longer Shorts.
7. **Metadata:**
   - A clear, searchable title that names the tool or problem (e.g. "Free Windows cleaner").
   - 1–3 relevant hashtags. #shorts is optional; never anywhere near 60.
   - Set the **Related video** link to a matching long-form video when one exists, and mention it on screen.
8. **Post at a steady, sustainable pace.** Quality beats count, and time of day doesn't matter. Keep the user's spacing between uploads, and never batch-dump near-identical videos.
9. **Never delete and re-upload a Short that underperformed.** It can take off days later, and re-uploading risks a spam flag. Make a genuinely new version instead.
10. **Judge Shorts by engaged views, stayed-to-watch rate, subscribers gained and related-link clicks.** Ignore the inflated raw view counts (since Mar 2025 / Aug 2026). Compare each Short against the channel's own recent Shorts; YouTube publishes no benchmarks.

## How a faceless, AI-run YouTube Shorts channel grows fastest (as of 2026-11-20)

I found no controlled public experiment on Shorts growth tactics. Every source is marked with one of these evidence labels:
- **[Official]**: YouTube policy, help page or statement.
- **[Large obs.]**: a vendor analysis of many channels. It shows correlation, not cause.
- **[Academic]**: peer-reviewed.
- **[Survey]**: creators' answers to a questionnaire.
- **[Self-rep.]**: one creator reporting their own numbers.
- **[Blog]**: opinion or vendor marketing.

Each tactic is also marked **Safe**, **Grey** or **Restricted**.

### Tactics ranked by expected subscriber gain

**1. Hook and "viewed vs swiped away" (Safe, biggest lever)**
- **What YouTube says.** Studio reports "Viewed (vs. swiped away)" for every Short [Official] (support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281). YouTube says each Short is first shown to a small seed audience and spreads only if that group engages [Official, TechCrunch 2023-08-25]. YouTube publishes no target numbers.
- **Since 2025-03-31, a view counts as soon as a Short starts or replays.** The older measure is now called "engaged views", and monetization still uses it. Use engaged views when comparing Shorts [Official] (techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted).
- **Practical advice [Blog].** Put the payoff in frame 1, for example the "after" state of a cleaner or an FPS counter. Re-hook every 3-5 seconds. End the video so it flows back into the opening, which earns replays. I found no published data on loops.

**2. Volume and consistency (Safe; Restricted if it slides into near-duplicates)**
- **Posting more correlates with growth.** vidIQ studied 10.2M channels (2026-07-08). Median monthly subscriber growth was:

| Uploads per month | Median subscriber growth |
|---|---|
| Under 1 | 0.00% |
| 4-7 | 0.39% |
| 12 or more | 0.91% |

  It does not separate Shorts from long videos [Large obs.] (vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study).
- **New channels: the first Shorts don't get better with practice.** vidIQ studied 448k channels (2026-07-28). Median 30-day views were 1,362 for a channel's 1st Short and 1,344 for its 10th. The best of the first 10 Shorts reached a median of 8,832 views. 48% of channels had a 10k-view Short within their first 10 [Large obs.] (vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study). Volume buys more chances at a breakout; it does not lift the average Short.
- **The limit.** YouTube's Spam policy bans using AI or automated tools to flood the platform with "high volumes of similar content with minimal changes" [Official] (support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973).

**3. Narrow the niche, then turn hits into series and "part 2" (Safe)**
- Kapwing's faceless channel narrowed its topic after one 200k-view Short. Repeating that pattern ("Hardest routine on __", biopic series) took it to 1,000 subscribers [Self-rep.] (kapwing.com/resources/we-grew-a-faceless-youtube-channel-to-1000-subscribers, 2025-01-06).
- No study measures "part 2" Shorts. The logic is sound: a series gives viewers a reason to subscribe.

**4. Search-intent titles (Safe)**
- Kapwing says titles written as questions people search kept bringing traffic for months [Self-rep.].
- For this channel that means titles like "How to lower input delay Windows 11", "Free crosshair overlay" or "Debloat Windows in 30s".
- Hashtags (#Shorts plus 3-5 tags) are weak [Blog]. Links in Shorts descriptions have not been clickable since 2023-08-31 [Official, searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463].

**5. Subscribe CTA (Safe; works if it comes late and short)**
- Evidence for:
  - A long-form test found that without a CTA, fewer than 1 in 1,500 viewers subscribed, and a strong spoken CTA converted far more [Blog] (castfromclay.co.uk/how-to/increase-youtube-subscription-rates-by-200x).
  - Kapwing says a CTA matters even more for faceless channels [Self-rep.].
  - Journalism.co.uk reports a CTA lifted the subscriber rate without hurting virality [Self-rep.].
- Evidence against: one unsourced claim that a CTA in the intro costs 15-20% of viewers [Blog].
- Verdict: a single spoken line after the payoff, tied to a series ("part 3 tomorrow, follow"). Never in the first 3 seconds.

**6. Related-video link and a Shorts-to-long-form funnel (Safe)**
- Each Short can link one video (long video, live or Short) in Studio under "Related video" [Official, 2023] (tubefilter.com/2023/09/01/youtube-related-links-multi-format-creators-shorts-live-long-form).
- AIR Media-Tech claims the link plus a spoken CTA lifts Shorts-to-long-form click-through from 0.04% to 0.5%. It gives no method [Blog].
- AIR's 18k-channel study (Mar 2025-Mar 2026) found mixed channels beat long-only ones in tech-adjacent niches (business +39%, education +37%). Gaming and science went the other way. The best mix was 25-40% Shorts [Large obs.] (air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels).

**7. Length (Safe)**
- Adobe surveyed 507 creators in March 2026 (published 2026-06-24). 53% said under 30 seconds is best for views. For subscriber growth, under 30s and 30-60s tied at 34% each [Survey] (adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study).
- Shorts can run up to 3 minutes since 2024-10-15. But a Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked, and Shorts-library songs are only allowed under 1 minute [Official] (blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts; support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877).
- Default to 20-45 seconds. Use 60-90 seconds only for full demos, with royalty-free audio.

**8. Trending audio and remixes (Safe within the audio library)**
- No data isolates the effect for creators. YouTube's "Shorts tripled artist audiences" figure is about musicians, not creators [Official].
- For screen demos, the gain is small next to the hook.

**9. Collabs (Safe, hard for a faceless channel)**
- YouTube's co-author feature (announced 2025-08-01) shows the video to both channels' audiences [Official] (ppc.land/youtube-launches-collaboration-feature-for-creator-partnerships).
- Sources disagree on the cap (5 or 10 creators), and I could not confirm whether it works on Shorts.
- Best use here: co-author with Krypt's other channels or tool partners.

**10. Replying to comments, hearts, pinned comment (Safe if human-sounding; Grey if templated)**
- Shorts get more likes per view but fewer comments per view than regular videos. That comes from 9.9M Shorts across ~70k channels (Violot et al., WebSci'24) [Academic] (arxiv.org/abs/2403.00454).
- There is no data showing replies or hearts lift reach [Blog].
- Pin a question that asks for an opinion ("which tool next?"). That feeds ideas for the series.
- The Spam policy bans "high-volume, repetitive" comments [Official].

**11. Commenting on other channels (Grey, low gain)**
- Spam policy item 3 applies. Reply only with real, varied comments, a few a day.

**12. Playlists and channel page (Safe, low gain)**
- Neither has a measured effect for Shorts.
- Still cheap to do: one playlist per tool, a sections layout (Popular Shorts, then the long guides), and a channel trailer for non-subscribers.

**13. Posting time (Safe, minor)**
- Adobe's survey favours Friday/Saturday and 6-8pm [Survey].
- Kapwing saw Shorts pick up again weeks after posting [Self-rep.]. Don't delete slow starters.

### Realistic numbers (none are controlled data)

- **Subscribers per 1,000 views:**
  - The commonly cited average is ~1.7 (16.9 per 10k). It traces to a single unverified blog post [Blog] (research.stripo.email/youtube-benchmarks).
  - Kapwing's channel works out to ~1.1 (1.2k subs from 1.1M views) [Self-rep., derived].
  - A Skool post reports 42.87k subs from 12.77M views in 90 days, about 3.4 [Self-rep.].
  - Planning range: **0.5-3**, with ~1-2 typical. How-to and utility topics should convert toward the top of that range (my inference). Since March 2025 public view counts are inflated, so measure per 1,000 *engaged* views.
- **New faceless Shorts channel posting 1-2 a day.** My estimates below combine vidIQ's medians with these conversion rates:

| Point | Typical | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Day 30 | 20-150 subs | 30-60 Shorts at a ~1.3k median |
| Day 90 | 150-800 subs | |
| Day 180 | ~500-2,000 subs | Kapwing reached 1k on day 157 [Self-rep.]. A LinkedIn case had 640 at 6 months [Self-rep.]. |

- **Outliers [Self-rep.]:** 4,200 subs in 30 days (nemovideo.com/blog/create-faceless-youtube-videos-ai-2026); 42.87k in 90 days (Skool). The whole range depends on one or two breakout Shorts. vidIQ's best-of-10 median was ~6.5x the first Short.

### Mass-growth playbook for this channel

**Before posting**
- Handle and name built around the theme (PC tools / AI bots). The description names each tool.
- Channel links to the site; Shorts description links don't click, so put them there.
- Sections: Popular Shorts, then long guides (Best Windows Tweaks), then one playlist per tool.
- Bank 15-20 Shorts across 5 formats:
  1. before/after (cleaner, tweaker)
  2. "you're doing X wrong" (crosshair, macro)
  3. speed demo (editor)
  4. bot result reveal, honest and with no return promises
  5. narrated reel
- Every Short has its own script, footage and hook. No template batches with swapped words.
- Set the related video on each Short to the matching long guide.

**Daily**
- Post 2-3 Shorts at least 2 hours apart (the user's set gap). Each one gets a search-phrase title and a late, one-line, series-tied CTA.
- Pin one question comment per Short.
- Reply in a human way to early comments during the first hours. Respect the hub's 2-per-thread cap.
- At most a few real comments on peer channels.

**Weekly**
- One long video every week or two, built from the best-performing Short's topic. Point all related Shorts at it.
- Review each format on Shorts 7 or more days old (late bumps are common):
  - viewed %
  - average percentage viewed
  - engaged views
  - subscribers per 1,000 engaged views
  - related-video clicks
- Refresh playlists and the channel trailer.

**When to drop or double a format**
- Judge a format after 10 uploads, using medians not single hits.
- **Drop** it if viewed % and median engaged views are both below the channel's median and subs per 1,000 engaged views are under ~0.5.
- **Double** it when a Short reaches about 5x the channel median, or converts at 2x the channel's subscriber rate. Post "part 2" within 24-48 hours, then a 3-5 part series, then a long version linked as the related video.
- **Kill on sight:** any format that drifts into template sameness, repost-style clips or "easy money" claims.
- Re-check "Account status" and monetization notices every week.

## YouTube Shorts to downloads: research report (as of 2026-11-20)

**The main point:** a Short cannot carry a clickable link to krypt.cc. Every route to a download takes at least two taps, or the viewer has to type the address. Nobody has published controlled data on Shorts-to-download conversion. The only rates I found are vendor guesses, and they disagree by about 100x. So rank the tactics by mechanics, and measure your own numbers from day one.

Evidence labels used below: **Official** (YouTube or Google help pages), **Vendor** (a company selling tools or services), **Survey** (self-reported), **Anecdote**, **None**.

**First step:** check Studio > Settings > Channel > Feature eligibility. Without advanced features (from channel history, or phone plus ID/video verification), nearly every clickable route above is closed.

### 2. Tactics ranked by expected customers

1. **Short, then related video, then a long-form demo or tutorial with krypt.cc in the first line of the description.** This is the only one-tap path out of a Short.
   - Evidence: vendor numbers that conflict:
     - air.io (18,000 channels, correlational): Shorts-to-long-form goes "from 0.04% to 0.5%" once the related video is set, method not stated ([updated 2026-05-28](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)).
     - subscribr.ai: related-video clicks are "sometimes under 1%", 1–3% with a contextual call to action ([link](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)).
     - longstories.ai: about 5% ([link](https://longstories.ai/blog/shorts-boost-long-form-video-performance)).
   - An academic study of 250 creators found Shorts did not significantly reduce long-form views for Shorts-first creators. It did not measure link clicks ([arXiv 2402.18208](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18208v2)).
   - Strength: weak. Still ranked first because the long video also gets found in search on its own.
2. **Long-form tutorials that people search for** ("reduce input lag Windows 11", "best free crosshair overlay"), with the Shorts feeding them. Viewers who search have intent and get a clickable description link. Evidence: none specific to software. This is mechanics and logic.
3. **Say and show the domain** ("krypt.cc", spoken plus on screen for the whole Short, plus "search Krypt"). The only measured data is Google's Attributed Branded Searches, which covers *ads*, not organic Shorts. Google's ABCD/Kantar study (Apr 2021, about 11,000 ads, Google-commissioned) found up to +30% short-term sales likelihood for "brand early, often" ([Google ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551)). Strength: vendor-official, and for ads only. You can measure it yourself through the krypt.cc direct-traffic share.
4. **"Link on my channel"** (tap the handle, then the channel page, then the link). Three taps, no data. Low cost, so do it.
5. **Posts with a link** to a new release or demo. Reaches subscribers only. No data.
6. **Pinned comment with the URL as text.** Not clickable on Shorts, so it works only as a copy-able reminder. Low.
7. **Shopping, brand link sticker:** not available for your own software.

### 3. Content that sells software

- **Demo-first** (the result on screen in the first 1–2 seconds, the tool's UI right after): Wyzowl says 80% have "bought or downloaded an app after watching an app demo video". That is a survey of 266 respondents including marketers, late 2025 ([Wyzowl 2026](https://wyzowl.com/video-marketing-statistics-2020)). Weak but consistent.
- **Before/after** (FPS, latency, startup time, disk freed): no controlled data. It is the natural proof format for a tweaker or cleaner. Only show real, reproducible numbers (see the risks below).
- **Comparisons with paid tools** (the versus style): no data for organic Shorts. Prices must be sourced and dated.
- **Tutorials:** these are the long-form landing pages. Search intent makes them the strongest closer (logic, no data).
- **"Tool I wish I knew" / list formats:** no data. Fine as hooks.
- **Testimonials:** no data. Only real ones with permission. Fabricated reviews violate policy and the law.
- **Sound:** Google says voice or music in Shorts *ads* lifts conversions by more than 20% ([Shorts ads guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697)). Vendor-official, ads only.
- **Apps generally:** Sweatcoin saw +20% new users from Shorts *ads* to a landing page. Agency self-report ([Kurve](https://kurve.co.uk/youtube-shorts-web-to-app-tracking)).
- **Organic SaaS:** anecdotes only (for example, one Reddit founder got a first paying customer from a single Short).

### 4. Calls to action: what works and what gets flagged

- **Allowed:** "full tutorial: tap the video below my name", "krypt.cc", "search Krypt tweaker", "link's on my channel", asking for comments, likes and subscribes. Asking for engagement is fine. *Paying or rewarding* people for it is not ([Spam policy 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)).
- **Risky or against policy:**
  - "Content created solely to drive users off YouTube".
  - "high-volume, repetitive or deceptive comments… to drive traffic".
  - "Using tools or AI to flood the platform with near-identical content".
  - Misleading titles or thumbnails.
  - Pay-per-click referral links in comments.
  - The External links policy treats spoken URLs and spellings like "dot com" the same as clickable links ([9054257](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9054257)).
- **"Comment and I'll reply with the link":** YouTube has no DMs, links in Shorts comments are dead, and identical replies containing a link are a spam signal. Reply without the link ("it's on my channel"), vary the wording, and keep the hub's 2-per-thread cap.
- **Link holding:** comments containing links get auto-held by spam filters. The channel owner's own comments are exempt from the "Block links" setting ([9482362](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9482362)).

### 5. Policy for the products

- **Software links:** a link to anything Google considers "malware or unwanted software" gets removed (warning, then strikes; 3 strikes in 90 days can end the channel). Google's Unwanted Software Policy bans "misleading claims related to… system performance, system optimization" and "false or misleading claims about the state of the user's system" ([policy](https://www.google.com/about/unwanted-software-policy.html)). For a tweaker or cleaner, never say things like "your PC is infected or slow", and keep downloads clean and unbundled. Unsigned installers raise the odds of a Safe Browsing or SmartScreen flag (inference, not documented).
- **Macro and crosshair:** branded content bans "hacking software" ([Branded Content Policies 17596007](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/17596007)). Never frame these as cheats or anti-cheat bypasses.
- **Polymarket bots:**
  - YouTube has **no rule that names prediction markets** (none found).
  - Since 2025-03-19, YouTube removes content steering viewers to *gambling* sites "not approved by Google", by link, logo or voice. It removes content "promising guaranteed returns… regardless of whether the site is certified", and age-restricts gambling promotion ([9229611](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229611); [policy updates](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10008196)).
  - Nov 2025 extended this to wagering with digital goods.
  - Google *Ads* treats prediction markets as their own category, certified, US only, from 2026-01-21, with states excluded (Nevada, later Michigan, New York, Ohio) ([adspolicy 16757872](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16757872)).
  - The scam rules ban "get rich quick" schemes.
  - In practice: no profit or ROI claims and no P&L screenshots. Link to krypt.cc, not to Polymarket. Expect age restriction (reach drops) if a moderator reads it as gambling. Branded content in finance or gambling needs a Google-certified partner.
- **Paid promotion box:** YouTube's endorsement definition includes "a creator's own brands if the relationship between creator and brand is not clear." A channel clearly branded Krypt promoting Krypt is arguably exempt; an unbranded channel should tick the box (official wording, my interpretation). Since Sep 2026 YouTube auto-detects undeclared promotion and may label it. Creator-program payouts and affiliate links (ElevenLabs) always need disclosure.
- **AI channel:** the YPP "inauthentic content" rename (2025-07-15) targets mass-produced, repetitive videos. AI use alone is allowed ([TechCrunch 2025-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows)).

### 6. Measuring

- **UTM tags** on every description, channel and post link: `krypt.cc/tweaker?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=longform&utm_campaign=<videoID>`. Use a separate `utm_medium=channel` or `post` for those links. Better still, use a short path per tool (krypt.cc/yt) that counts hits and redirects, because spoken URLs carry no UTM.
- **On the site:** count download clicks and installs per source, and watch the direct-traffic share for the domain-on-screen effect.
- **YouTube Analytics:** I found **no official per-Short "related video clicks" metric**. Proxy: on the long video, Traffic source "Shorts" ("traffic from the Shorts vertical viewing experience", [9314355](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355)), divided by the linked Short's views. Also track per-Short subscribers gained, shown in feed vs. chose to view, and long-form description link clicks through UTM sessions.

### "Push product" playbook

**Funnel:** Short (demo result in the first 2 seconds, krypt.cc on screen the whole time, spoken call to action "full setup: tap the video under my name") → related video → 3–8 min long-form tutorial (krypt.cc/tool?utm… as the first description line, pinned comment, end screen to the next tool) → krypt.cc download page → install. Back-ups: the channel links, with krypt.cc first, and a weekly post.

**Setup (once):**
- Unlock advanced features.
- Make one long-form tutorial per tool, six in all: tweaker, cleaner, crosshair, macro, editor, Polymarket bot "AI trades for you" walkthrough (no P&L).
- Write the channel description and the 1–5 channel links.

**Daily:**
- Post 1–2 Shorts at the hub's spacing, each with its related video set to that tool's tutorial. Formats: before/after, comparison with a paid tool, a "one setting" tip, a demo.
- Write titles that people search.
- Reply to comments in human-sounding text without links, within the reply caps.
- Write unique descriptions; no copy-paste.

**Weekly:**
- One new long-form tutorial or comparison.
- One post linking the newest tool.
- Review the numbers below and rotate formats. Retire any format that sits below the channel median on downloads per 1,000 views for two weeks.

**Measure (weekly):**
- Per Short: views, chose-to-view %, subscribers gained, and the linked long video's "Shorts" traffic (that gives the bridge rate).
- Per long video: UTM sessions and downloads.
- Site-wide: downloads per 1,000 Shorts views by tool and format, and the direct-traffic share.

**Main risks for an AI agent:**
1. Spam flags from near-identical uploads, descriptions or link comments, and channel-level AI mass-production enforcement.
2. Profit wording on Polymarket content leading to removal, a strike, or a gambling age restriction. Never link to Polymarket.
3. Tweaker or cleaner overclaims, or an unsigned binary flagged as unwanted software, getting links stripped or videos removed.
4. Macro or crosshair framed as cheats.
5. Missing paid promotion or affiliate disclosure, now auto-detected.
6. The agent assuming a link is clickable on a Short when it is not, which wastes the call to action.
7. Changing the related video or links in bulk through Studio, which may look automated. Pace edits.
8. Recording real wallet or account data in demos.

**Gaps:** no controlled data on Shorts-to-download rates, any per-Short related-video click metric, prediction-market rules on YouTube itself, or software support in Shopping. Your own UTM numbers within 2–4 weeks will be the first real evidence.

YouTube Shorts: limits, warm-up and restriction risks for a new AI-run channel (researched 2026-11-20)

Labels: [OFFICIAL] means YouTube Help or policy text, [REPORTED] means several independent sources agree, [ANECDOTAL] means one creator, forum post or vendor.

## 1. Upload limits and feature tiers
- [OFFICIAL] A channel has a daily upload limit that covers desktop, mobile and the API together. YouTube does not publish the number. The error says "Daily upload limit reached. You can upload more videos in 24 hours." Limits vary by country and channel history, and "Community Guidelines strikes will affect how much you can upload." (support.google.com/youtube/answer/10383400, answer/57407, fetched 2026-11-20)
- [OFFICIAL] Studio uploads up to 15 files at a time. That is a batch size, not a daily cap (answer/57407).
- [OFFICIAL] Feature tiers (answer/9890437):
  - **Standard** comes with a new channel and has no active strikes. It allows videos, Shorts, playlists and posts, each with a "limited daily limit".
  - **Intermediate** needs phone verification. It adds videos over 15 minutes, live streaming from a computer, custom thumbnails, podcasts and higher limits.
  - **Advanced** comes from "sufficient channel history" (not defined) or ID/video verification. It adds clickable links in long-form descriptions and posts, pinned comments, chapters, monetization applications, A/B tests, "additional channels" and higher daily limits. You keep it only with a clean record.
- [OFFICIAL] Unverified accounts are capped at 15 minutes per video. Verified accounts go up to 12 hours or 256 GB (answer/71673).
- [REPORTED, unverified numbers] Newer channels hit roughly 10 to 20 uploads a day. Established channels hit roughly 50 to 100. One creator hit the cap at 10 uploads in 24 hours on a brand-new unverified channel (socialrails.com, updated 2026-07-20; lilachbullock.com 2026; fluxnote.io 2026-03-04).
- [OFFICIAL, API only] The Data API defaults to 10,000 quota units a day, and an upload costs about 1,600 units. This does not apply to Studio browser uploads.

## 2. How new channels get flagged or terminated
- [OFFICIAL] **Spam, deceptive practices and scams policy** (answer/2801973) bans:
  - "high-volume, repetitive, or deceptive comments… to drive traffic"
  - using automated tools or AI to make "high volumes of similar content with minimal changes"
  - scraped or re-posted content with no added value
  - off-platform diversion
  - technical manipulation to get past filters
- [OFFICIAL] **Fake engagement policy** (answer/3399767) bans "anything that artificially increases the number of views, likes, comments, or other metrics", automatic systems, and sub4sub. Asking viewers to like or subscribe is fine. Artificial traffic isn't counted and can lead to strikes. Violations by someone you hire still count against your channel.
- [OFFICIAL] **Terms of Service** (effective 2023-12-15, youtube.com/t/terms):
  - No access "using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" without written permission.
  - You may not "cause or encourage any inaccurate measurements of genuine user engagement."
  - This means an AI agent driving a browser is technically outside the ToS, whatever the volume. The practical risk is in behaviour that looks automated, not in the browser itself.
- [OFFICIAL] **Inauthentic (was "repetitious") content**, clarified 2025-07-15: this is a monetization (YPP) rule. Mass-produced or template content can lose monetization across the whole channel (YouTube via Search Engine Journal and Social Media Today, July 2025).
- [OFFICIAL] **Strikes vs termination** (answer/2802032):
  - The first violation is usually a warning. Policy training lets it expire after 90 days.
  - Strike 1 blocks uploads, posts and custom thumbnails for one week. Strike 2 blocks posting for two weeks. Strike 3 within 90 days means termination.
  - "A single case of severe abuse" (spam is listed) ends the channel with no warning. So does a channel dedicated to violations.
- [OFFICIAL] **Associated accounts and circumvention** (answer/2802168): after a termination you may not "use, possess, or create any other YouTube channels." This covers new or acquired channels and channels you appear in often. Re-uploading removed content also counts as circumvention. Appeals are open for up to one year. Since 2025-10-08 a pilot lets some terminated creators request a new channel after one year.
- [REPORTED] October to November 2025 wave: channels were terminated for "spam, deceptive practices and scams" or for being "linked" to other removed channels. Appeals came back fast and templated. Some channels were restored only after public outcry. YouTube said most terminations were correct and that "a scammy account can have hundreds of channels." Google researchers described cluster-based enforcement that took down 130K channels in 50K clusters over six months. That is the mechanism by which one bad channel can sink its siblings (Search Engine Journal; ppc.land; Outlook Respawn, Nov 2025).

## 3. Comments and likes
- [OFFICIAL] Comments can be held or removed for:
  - "Posting large volumes of comments in a short amount of time"
  - repeating the same text
  - links
  - heavy emoji or unusual characters
- [OFFICIAL] Commenting can be paused on an account. Channels hold "potentially inappropriate" comments by default, and creators choose Basic, Strict or Hold all. Comments from hidden users never show in the creator's Studio (answer/13209064, answer/9482362).
- [OFFICIAL] A 24-hour comment timeout applies after repeated violating comments (YouTube via 9to5google, 2022-12-15).
- [ANECDOTAL] No published "comment posting limit" number exists. Users report:
  - short cool-downs after bursts
  - being unable to post more than about 2 comments on a video
  - comments vanishing with no trace (likely shadow-hidden or spam-filtered)
  - new accounts with no watch history being filtered more often
- [OFFICIAL / ANECDOTAL] Likes from fake or automated activity are discounted (the fake engagement policy says artificial traffic "isn't counted"). Reports that likes from new accounts silently don't count are anecdotal.

## 4. Does a warm-up help?
- [ANECDOTAL / REPORTED] YouTube has no official warm-up concept. Warm-ups matter less than on TikTok or Instagram, because Shorts are pushed by per-video viewer response more than account trust. They still help in two ways:
  - building a believable Google account and watch history lowers spam-filter and "unusual activity" risk
  - comment visibility seems to improve on accounts with real history
- [REPORTED] Advice for new channels runs 1 to 2 Shorts a day, rising to 2 to 3 and then 3 to 5 as the channel grows. Over 5 to 10 a day is widely said to split views. The vidIQ July 2026 study of 10.2M channels found growth rose with upload frequency, but that is correlation. No source shows that frequency itself causes penalties. Spam flags come from repetitiveness.

**Suggested 7-day plan for a brand-new channel (not official):**
- **Day 0:** Create the channel. Phone-verify it, because that unlocks Intermediate access, custom thumbnails and long videos, and makes the account look human. Add an avatar, banner and a plain description with no links.
- **Days 1 to 2:** Spend 20 to 40 minutes a day watching niche Shorts and long videos, scrolling the Shorts feed with natural skips. Like 5 to 10 videos. Subscribe to 2 to 3 channels. No comments.
- **Days 3 to 4:** Same watching. Like 10 to 15 videos and subscribe to 3 to 5 channels. Post 2 to 3 short, specific comments on mid-size videos, with no links or emoji walls.
- **Day 5:** First upload, one Short, done through Studio by hand-like steps (original title, no links). Keep watching as usual afterwards.
- **Days 6 to 7:** One Short a day, 3 to 5 comments a day, and reply to every comment on your own posts.
- **Weeks 2 to 4:** Week 2 is 1 to 2 Shorts a day. Weeks 3 to 4 are 2 to 3 a day, spaced at least 2 hours apart. Raise volume only while there are no held or removed comments and no warnings.
- **Already-active channel:** Keep its current cadence. Raise it by at most about 1 extra Short a day per week, and raise comments by about 25 to 50% a week.

## 5. Safe daily volumes (unofficial, aggregated; not YouTube numbers)

| Account age | Uploads (Shorts) | Comments + replies | Likes | Subscriptions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-4 (no uploads) | 0 | 0-3 | 5-15 | 2-5 |
| Week 1 | 1 | 3-5 | 10-20 | 3-5 |
| Weeks 2-4 | 1-2, rising to 2-3 | 5-15 | 20-40 | 5-10 |
| Months 1-3, clean record | 2-4 | 15-30 | 40-80 | 10-15 |
| 3+ months / Advanced | 3-5 (10+ is possible but hurts reach) | 30-50, spread across hours | 80-150 | 15-25 |

Basis: vendor and creator guidance (fluxnote 2026, socialrails 2026, Hopper HQ "max 3 posts/day until mature"), plus the official triggers above (volume in short windows, repetition, links).

## 6. Several channels from one Google account or one Chrome profile
- [OFFICIAL] One Google account can own several channels through Brand Accounts, one channel per Brand Account. "Additional channels" is now listed as an Advanced feature, so a new, unverified account may not be able to add channels yet (answer/9890437, answer/1646861).
- [OFFICIAL] A termination blocks every channel you own or appear in often. A strike-related ban is also circumvented by using another channel.
- [REPORTED] Cluster or "linked" enforcement can terminate siblings that share an owner, login, device or IP. One spam-flagged channel can take down every channel on that Google account.
- [ANECDOTAL] Switching channels inside one Chrome profile is normal creator behaviour and isn't flagged on its own. The risk comes from sharing a login and device fingerprint. Separate Google accounts in separate Chrome profiles limit the damage, but separate accounts used to evade enforcement count as circumvention.

## 7. Hard rules for an AI agent running a YouTube channel in a browser
1. Use only a real, signed-in Chrome profile with normal timing. No headless runs, no Studio API scraping, no parallel tabs posting at once.
2. Phone-verify before the first upload. Never attempt more than 10 uploads a day on a channel under 30 days old. Stop for 24 hours on "Daily upload limit reached."
3. Space Shorts at least 2 hours apart. Each one gets its own title, description and hashtags, and the footage and narration must differ meaningfully. No templated near-duplicates.
4. Add no links in comments, and none in Short descriptions until the channel has Advanced access. Keep descriptions honest and free of misleading clickbait.
5. Comments must be short, specific and human-sounding. Never paste the same text twice. Limit yourself to 2 comments per thread. Never burst more than about 5 comments in 10 minutes.
6. Watch before engaging: view at least part of a video before liking or commenting on it, and do it inside a real watch session.
7. No sub4sub, no "like for like", no rewards for engagement, no bought views, likes or subscribers, and no engagement between linked accounts of the same owner.
8. If a comment is held or vanishes, or commenting is paused, or a warning or strike arrives, stop all engagement for 24 to 72 hours. Report it to the user and never retry around it.
9. Never re-upload removed content. Never create or use another channel after a strike or termination, because that is circumvention.
10. Keep each channel on its own Brand Account. Treat one Google account as one blast radius: a spam flag on one channel can end all of them.

## Patterns among the winners

1. **High volume.** The growers post 24–48 times in 28 days: Astral 24, SetupsAI 29, Hone 32, TechSimplify 48. Channels at 0–7 uploads are flat (Trilzo, Serg Tech, TroubleChute).
2. **One concrete result per Short, named in the title.** "Check SSD health without any app", "Find the hidden apps eating your PC storage".
3. **"Without upgrading / without any app / for free" framing** drives the biggest Windows and FPS hits. "4 FREE Ways To Boost FPS" from META PCs has 2.9M views.
4. **Riding a news peg.** GTA 6 on PS5 gave Astral 1M views. Hone makes one Short per new game, so each search finds a fresh one.
5. **Repeatable title templates.** "...you didn't know", "I guarantee you didn't know", "change this right now", and "3"/"5" lists.
6. **Numbered series build a habit but wear out.** Itsreeeda is at part 241 and slowing.
7. **Product channels judge by views, not subs.** Hone has 95M views on 23K subs, and the first line of its description is the download link.
8. **A long-form anchor for search.** CyberCPU and Chris Titus Tech earn on evergreen tutorials. I did not verify whether specific Shorts link to them through the related-video feature.
9. **One signature emoji, used every time** (Astral 😳, exm 😲🚀), with otherwise plain titles.
10. **Real screens, specific settings.** None of the growers I checked are generic AI-generated stock footage.

## What has stalled or been punished

- **Big but flat channels:**
  - Brett In Tech: 1.42M subs, 0 net gain Aug–Oct.
  - Trilzo: 325K, no uploads.
  - Serg Tech: 589K, flat.
  - TroubleChute: 300K, flat.
  - ADOPIX: 989K, flat.
- **Signs of inflated subscriber counts in AI-tool and FPS channels:**
  - Bella AI & Tech: 691K subs on a channel that started 2026-08-18, best video 31K views, −4K in 28 days.
  - TRONZ GAMING: 333K subs, 1.15M lifetime views.
  - Techify: 252K subs, 3.0M views.
  - AiZen Tools: 372K subs, 2.06M views (Playboard).

  These look like bought subscribers or sponsored-review farms. Don't benchmark against them.
- **Saturated AI-tool hooks.** A this-month search for "ai tool nobody talks about" returned 14 Shorts, nearly all under 1K views and many under 100. AI trading-bot Shorts ("simulated AI trade +13.9%") sit at 0–12 views.
- **YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule (July 15, 2025).** It is the old repetitious-content rule renamed. It covers mass-produced, template content and applies to the whole channel, not single videos.
- **AI slop crackdown (early 2026).** YouTube removed 16 top AI-slop channels with 35M subscribers and 4.7B views. Separately, reports say an automated Google system terminated about 130K spam channels in six months; that figure comes from a single outlet.
- **Gambling rules (March 19, 2025).** YouTube bans links or verbal pointers to unapproved gambling sites, and removes "guaranteed returns" content even when the site is approved. Betting promotion is age-restricted. How prediction markets are treated is unclear.
- **Fake prediction-market wins.**
  - A WSJ investigation found about 70% of Polymarket's paid creator videos showed bets that never happened, staged on copycat sites: 118 videos claimed about $900K in made-up winnings, and the clips drew 140M+ views.
  - Semafor (July 2026) reported internal training videos for those creators.
  - Separately, "AI quant bot" copy-trade scams route viewers to Telegram.
