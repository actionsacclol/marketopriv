### Patterns that repeat

1. **Two short-form lengths work. The middle does not.**
   - About 10-15 s, silent, one visual contrast with a payoff (neuronvisuals, the early Ricks Clips gags).
   - About 45-65 s, narrated curiosity explainer with a "Why… / How… / The X that…" title (Ricks Clips now).
   - 1:40-3:00 "Part N" cuts do badly (Beanie: 1.5-31K).
2. **Most of the 2026 stickman wave grows on long-form, not Shorts.** Bradikinin, Ifverse, Beanie and Casually Explained all post Shorts as trailers or clips, and those get 1-10% of their long-form views. A stickman look alone does not carry a Short. The idea and the opening do.
3. **Fixed title templates and series names.** "If Your X Ever… DON'T Y", "What If You…", "Psychology of…", "X Be Like", "S1E1 / Day 90". The repeating title and series is the brand.
4. **The art is very simple and the same every time.**
   - White or grey figures on black/dark, or black on paper-white.
   - **One accent colour** marks the payoff (yellow, an orange hood, red type).
   - Expression comes from meme faces or poses, not detailed drawing.
   - The vendor guidance from ShortFast is a new drawing every 2-4 s and about 150 words per 60 s (unverified).
5. **Humour types:**
   - Relatable observation (ChainsFR)
   - Deadpan absurdity (Casually Explained)
   - Dread and rules (Bradikinin, Beanie)
   - Moral contrast, e.g. perfectionist vs. action-taker (neuronvisuals)
   - Wordless slapstick (Pencilmation)
6. **Narration:** one steady AI or human voice with word-timed captions, or no voice at all with a trending sound. A common viewer complaint about AI stickman videos is "limbs too long, only the head moves, monotone" ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/varghq/skills/stickman-video)).

### Accounts that use stickman to sell something

- **neuronvisuals:** sells a 72-lesson course and a brand plan the creator puts at $2,000+ a month (Postiz, unverified).
- **Story Stix:** sells paid promotions.
- **Missing:** I found no conversion or install data anywhere tying stickman content to app downloads.
- **Weak spot:** ShortFast says the style is a poor fit for *showing* real products.

### Signs of saturation and risk

- **The "ancient humans" stickman wave peaked in spring 2026 and fell 80-95%** (Zenn, Axen, Mack). Daily long uploads now pull about 5K views.
- **Tooling has flooded the format.** The kaomei stickman skill has 7.3K GitHub stars, plus Stickreel, ShortFast, StickTale, ViewGum and others. There was an "autumn 2026 stickman reels wave" on X.
- **YouTube's rules:**
  - The "inauthentic content" policy (Jul 15, 2025) targets template-based, repetitive content and narrated stories or slideshows with only surface differences.
  - Mohan's Jan 2026 letter made cutting "AI slop" a priority. Tubefilter reported about 16 channels with 35M subscribers wiped out.
- **Pay:** Shorts RPM is about $0.03-0.15 (one blog estimate).

### What fits our PC-tools / AI-tools niche

- **Use stickman for the problem, a real screen recording for the proof.** Example: a stick guy stuck at 40 FPS while the PC fan screams, then a cut to the real Tweaker or Cleaner run with before/after numbers. This avoids the "can't show products" weakness and the template look.
- **For IG and TikTok: a silent 10-15 s contrast**, e.g. "Default Windows guy vs. tweaked guy", with a yellow (or Krypt-colour) payoff and the tool name in the last 2 s.
- **For Shorts: a 45-60 s narrated "Why your PC…" / "How Windows secretly…" explainer**, voiced by Jarnathan, captions on, a new scene every 2-4 s.
- **Polymarket bots:** a stick guy asleep while "AI trades for you" works. This fits the "never the visuals" rule, with no bot UI shown.
- **Name a series** (e.g. "Windows Crimes #4"). Use our own original character.
- **Avoid the saturated topics** (ancient humans, "what if", survival rules).
- **Vary scripts and layouts** so nothing reads as templated.
- **Test before scaling.** None of this is proven for PC tools: no tech-niche stickman account with verified big numbers turned up. The "Stickman Explains" channels exist, but I found no stats for them.

## STICKMAN REPORT 2: Hooks and formats

**Method:** I pulled live YouTube search and channel data on 2026-11-21 (views, length and publish dates from the page data), plus creator and vendor guides. Vendor numbers are marked unverified. I could not get transcripts (caption fetch was blocked), so I could not measure words per second directly.

### What is winning right now (verified views)

**1. Wordless "X vs Y" side-by-side, 10-30 s, music only, one-line labels.** This is the biggest format of autumn 2026.
- STICK TO IT (22.4K subs): "Overthinker vs Doer: Same Stone", 25 s, **12.5M** in 3 weeks ([link](https://youtube.com/shorts/jpj3u8o8pv0)). "Can You Do Me a Quick Favor?", 13 s, **11.2M** ([link](https://youtube.com/shorts/1Yy3KW4pJv8)). "Taking Off a Band-Aid", 16 s, **3.6M**. "Fake Friend vs Real Friend", 62 s, **3.5M**.
- Visual Mindset (43K subs): "Motivation vs Discipline vs Obsession: penalties", 30 s, **8.7M** ([link](https://youtube.com/shorts/fRrDj9KUrR8)). "…Which one were you?", 25 s, **2.8M**.
- How it works: the same object, the same day, two or three labelled stickmen doing it differently, then a visible result. The description always ends "Which one are you? Be honest in the comments". The title states the setup, not the result.
- These channels' wins are uneven. Most of their uploads get 15K-180K, so it is a hit-or-miss lottery, not a steady formula.

**2. Narrated curiosity explainers, about 60 s (the "Ricks Clips" style).** Ricks Clips (203K subs) posts 2D stickmen on 3D-looking backgrounds with an AI voiceover, and almost every short gets 5-35M views. Examples: "The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire", 65 s, **35.3M**. "Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work", 65 s, **30.8M**. "The Job Nobody Wants in Japan", **18.9M**. "Why Cashiers Keep Winning The Lottery", **10M** ([channel list](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC_VeFWlNnRIg558hAeIpJ4w/shorts)).
- Titles are "Why X…", "How X worked", "The X that Y": a question the viewer can't answer.
- A tutorial copying the style says "true stories get 4x the views" (unverified, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M_1IGXSQX_g)).
- Warning: a wave of "make Ricks Clips-style stickman shorts with AI" tutorials hit 100K-620K views each in Oct-Nov 2026 (Mark Ai Guy, Brunoe AI, The AI Blueprint). Expect copycat saturation soon. YouTube's "inauthentic content" monetization rule (renamed July 2025, from memory) targets mass-produced lookalikes.

**3. POV / relatable comedy, 10-15 s, often no voice.** Examples:
- "The Judge Asked What He Stole…", 14 s, **592K** in 1 week ([link](https://youtube.com/shorts/vF5sYANW5KM))
- "POV: MY WIFE BE LIKE", 15 s, **120K** in 1 day
- "POV: Corporate Jobs Be Like", 99 s, **85K**
- "How Meat Was Cooked 10,000 Years Ago (Stickman Edition)", 12 s, **486K**
- Rico Animations meme shorts, 15 s, **13-34M** (older)

**4. "Types / levels" lists.** These work best when drawing-led: "Types Of Stickmen People Draw", 60 s, **3.0M**. Plain "types of people at the gym" uploads mostly sit at about 1K.

**5. Warning for our niche:** literal stickman tech explainers flop. Examples: "STICKMAN EXPLAINS DIFFERENCES BETWEEN PC PARTS", 39 s, 2.1K. "What is Ram and Rom?", 3.4K. "Stickman Explains: What is an API?", 301. "PC Gamers Waiting for GTA 6 Be Like", 1.1K. What wins is conflict, contrast or a mystery with a stickman in it, not a stickman pointing at labels. Pitch our topics as rivalries or puzzles, not as definitions.

### Hook rules (first 1-3 s)
- **Start mid-action, never a still greeting** ([Cliptude](https://blog.cliptude.com/how-to-create-stickman-animation-videos-for-youtube-2/)). Frame 1 already shows the two contrasting stickmen or the strange object.
- **The title or on-screen line is the question; the payoff is visual and lands at the end.** "Math Says You Can Never Reach the Door" (67 s, 63K) opens on the paradox, not on the topic.
- **Vendor benchmarks (unverified, and they conflict):**
  - hold 70-90% through second 3
  - a swipe-away rate under 30% is "healthy"
  - typical strong Shorts keep about 73% retention ([aibrify](https://aibrify.com/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/))
  - Compare against your own Studio baseline instead.

### Pacing, narration and sound
- **Length:** two clear peaks. Wordless versus or POV runs 10-30 s; narrated explainers run 56-65 s. Little wins in between.
- **Scene changes:** change the image every 2-4 s, which is 8-12 scenes per 30 s (vendor heuristic, [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-shorts-retention-tips-2026)). Wordless versus clips cut on the beat.
- **Voice speed:** about 150-180 wpm, roughly 2.5-3 words per second, so about 85 words for 30 s (vendor range, unverified).
- **Narration:**
  - Versus and POV hits are music plus labels with no voice. That suits muted viewing and needs no narration spend.
  - Explainers use one calm AI narrator, captions and sound effects.
  - Keep burned-in captions either way ([StickReel](https://stickreel.com/how-to-make-a-stickman-animation), vendor).
- **Music:** Visual Mindset credits trending phonk-style tracks ("Mortals Funk Remix"). Keep music at about 15-20% of the voice level when narrated (Cliptude).

### Why these get shared and saved
- Instagram ranks on watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach**, and sends weigh most for reaching non-followers (Mosseri 2025, via [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)).
- "Which one are you?" and "tag the friend who…" turn a clip into a DM to a friend. A curiosity explainer turns it into a save.
- **Product without an ad feel:** the product is the "doer's" move in the story, shown for 2-3 s as a real screen recording, not a pitch. The CTA is one quiet line ("Tweaker, link in bio"), and the last frame loops back to the opening.

### 12 concepts
They follow your rules: no wins or profit for the bots, a plain risk line, no bot screens, no scare claims, no "open source" or "free to use", no stories of the narrator using a tool.

1. **"Gamer A vs Gamer B: Same Stutter"** (versus, 18 s, music only)
   - Hook: one PC, two stickmen, the game freezes.
   - Beats: A shakes the monitor; A scrolls GPU prices and cries; B opens Tweaker (real screen recording, 3 s); B is back in the match while A is still shopping.
   - End: "Which one are you?" / "Tweaker, link in bio".
2. **"Every type of person when the game lags"** (types, 25 s, narrated)
   - Hook: "Lag hits. Six people, six reactions."
   - Beats: restarts the router 6 times; blames the teammate; closes Chrome tabs one by one; sits in Task Manager confused; runs Cleaner (screen recording).
   - End: "Tag the router guy."
3. **"Why PCs feel slower every year"** (curiosity, 55 s, narrated)
   - Hook: "Nobody touched it. So why is it slower?"
   - Beats: stickman city with a highway (the boot); each install parks a car on it (startup apps); traffic jam; the stickman tows cars away (Cleaner's startup list, screen recording).
   - End: "Startup list is in Task Manager too." / soft Cleaner line.
4. **"POV: Windows says 'Working on updates 1%'"** (POV comedy, 12 s)
   - Hook: the stickman's face as the update starts mid-match.
   - Beats: 1%, then 1%, then 1%; he ages a beard; the tip card reads "Settings > Windows Update > Active hours".
   - End: "Save this before your next ranked."
5. **"Levels of Windows users"** (levels, 30 s, narrated)
   - Hook: "Level 1 still right-clicks Copy."
   - Beats: Ctrl+C; Win+V clipboard history; Win+Shift+S snip; Win+. emoji panel; Level 10 lets a macro do the repeats (macro tool screen recording).
   - End: "Which level are you?"
6. **"40 roommates you never invited"** (mini-story, 35 s, narrated)
   - Hook: "He moved in alone. 40 roommates came with the house."
   - Beats: preinstalled apps as roommates eating snacks (RAM); one plays music nobody asked for; he hands out polite notices; the debloat tool's screen recording; a quiet room.
   - End: "Keep the ones you use." No threat wording.
7. **"Same aim, different crosshair"** (versus, 15 s, music)
   - Hook: two snipers, same target, gray map.
   - Beats: A's crosshair vanishes into the wall; A misses three times; B shows a bright overlay crosshair (crosshair tool screen recording); B tags the target.
   - End: "Can you even see yours?"
8. **"Overthinker vs Delegator: the 3 AM market"** (versus, 20 s, music plus labels)
   - Hook: two stickmen and one open prediction market at 3:00 AM.
   - Beats: the overthinker refreshes the odds, coffee number 4; the delegator hands a task card to a little robot stickman (no app screens) and goes to sleep; sunrise shows the overthinker asleep on the keyboard.
   - End label: "AI trades for you. Easy. It can lose too. Only use money you can lose."
9. **"How do people bet on the weather?"** (curiosity, 50 s, narrated)
   - Hook: "Someone is paying 62 cents for 'it rains tomorrow.'"
   - Beats: a YES crowd vs a NO crowd; the price slides as people buy; "62 cents = the crowd thinks 62%"; some let an AI place the trades (robot stickman, no screens).
   - End: "Easy to start, risky like any market. Link in bio."
10. **"3 types of prediction-market people"** (types, 25 s, narrated)
    - Hook: "Gut feeling, spreadsheet, or robot?"
    - Beats: the gut guy flips a coin; the spreadsheet guy drowns in tabs; the third stickman's robot does the clicking while he goes outside.
    - End: "AI trades for you. Still a risk." / "Which one are you?"
11. **"Why your mouse feels like ice"** (curiosity, 30 s, narrated)
    - Hook: the stickman's cursor skids across a frozen lake.
    - Beats: same hand speed, the cursor goes further when you move fast; this is "Enhance pointer precision" (acceleration); the stickman turns off the checkbox (real Windows screen recording); steady aim.
    - End: "Mouse settings > Additional mouse options." Product only if Tweaker really has this toggle.
12. **"The stickman who never restarted"** (escalating plus twist, 30 s, narrated)
    - Hook: "Day 47 without a restart."
    - Beats: tabs pile up as stickmen crowding a room; the fan becomes a jet engine; the desk lifts off; the twist: a 9-second restart and everything is calm.
    - End: "Shut down isn't a full restart with Fast Startup on. Restart weekly."

**Recommendation:** test both formats.
- Wordless versus (concepts 1, 7, 8) is cheap: no ElevenLabs spend and it works muted.
- Narrated curiosity (3, 9, 11) gives the savable explainer.
- Keep one recurring stickman pair so it reads as a series, not a lookalike of trending channels.
- Do not post literal stickman definition videos; those flop.

## STICKMAN REPORT 3: Production tooling

**Bottom line:** write our own small rigged stickman in TypeScript that draws onto the Canvas 2D context we already render to. Add two MIT libraries: perfect-freehand for the tapered ink strokes and rough.js for hand-drawn props. Every frame is computed from the time alone, so the output is the same every time and costs nothing per render. Don't use GSAP (licence risk), Remotion (paid per render), Spine (needs editor licences) or AI video (inconsistent and paid per clip).

**What I checked in our code (read only, nothing changed):**
- `src/render/export.ts` draws each frame with `drawReel(ctx, {t, frame})` on a 1080x1920 canvas. It encodes with **mediabunny/WebCodecs H.264**, not ffmpeg as the brief says.
- `src/render/char3d.ts` already has a mascot. Each of its frames is computed from the beat time, and it takes an action list (`shared/character.ts`: talk, point, present, shrug, shock, cheer, think, wave, facepalm, sad, laugh) plus a `talk` mouth value.
  - The mouth comes from syllable flaps on word timings (char3d.ts:623) or from the voice's loudness (`kip.ts` `mouthTrack`).
- The stickman can be one more renderer behind that same interface. The AI agent then keeps the action vocabulary it already knows.

### Recommended architecture

**1. Rig (about 15 joints, 2D forward kinematics)**
- Root (hips) position, then spine, chest, neck and head. Each arm is shoulder, elbow, hand; each leg is hip, knee, foot.
- A pose = root offset + about 12 joint angles + face settings.
- Blend poses by interpolating angles along the shortest path, never by moving joint positions directly.
- **Two-bone IK** (a simple formula) when a hand must hit a target: pointing at a chart, keys on a keyboard, a phone in the hand. Foot-lock IK during walks and weight shifts so feet never slide.

**2. Layers, added on top of each other per frame**
- base pose clip
- moving hold: a breathing sine and a slight sway, so the figure never freezes
- talk gestures fired on stressed or long words
- blinks: random but seeded from the reel id
- head nods on word starts
- secondary motion: a spring on the hands and head for follow-through and overshoot

**3. Scene script (JSON written by the AI; times tied to words, not seconds)**
```json
{"style":{"bg":"paper","ink":"#111","accent":"#ff4d2e","onTwos":true,"boil":1.2},
 "cast":[{"id":"a","x":0.35,"scale":1,"facing":1}],
 "beats":[
  {"at":{"word":0},"cam":{"shot":"medium","on":"a","move":"pushIn"},
   "set":["desk","monitor"],"screen":"clip:polybot.mp4",
   "acts":[{"who":"a","do":"sitType","face":"focused"}]},
  {"at":{"word":14},"acts":[{"who":"a","do":"point","target":"chart","face":"smirk"}],
   "props":[{"id":"chart","kind":"lineChart","trend":"up","drawOn":{"word":15,"to":19}}],
   "text":{"big":"+38%","at":{"word":16}}},
  {"at":{"word":27},"cam":{"shake":0.6},"acts":[{"who":"a","do":"shock"}]}]}
```
- Resolve `{"word":n}` with the ElevenLabs `/with-timestamps` character alignment (or our Whisper pass).
- Each action starts 2–4 frames before its word, which leaves room for the anticipation move.
- The renderer checks the script and replaces unknown poses or props with the nearest known one.

**4. Render cost**
- Drawing a stickman, props and captions should take a few ms per frame on Canvas 2D (estimate, not measured).
- 60 s at 30 fps is 1,800 frames, so encoding dominates. My estimate is about 15–45 s per 60 s video on a mid-range PC with hardware H.264. Measure this.
- No API cost beyond the ElevenLabs narration.

### Why it looks cheap vs good

**Cheap:**
- thin uniform 2 px lines
- linear tweens
- all limbs starting and stopping together
- dead-still holds
- feet sliding or floating
- a tiny figure on plain white
- many random colours
- every motion at full 30 fps, which looks like a PowerPoint puppet

**Good:**
- **Line weight:** thick, tapered, round-capped strokes (perfect-freehand), about 14–22 px at 1080 width. A bigger head, solid or bold outline.
- **Easing:**
  - ease-in-out between poses
  - **anticipation** (a small opposite move for 3–4 frames)
  - **overshoot** (easeOutBack-style, then settle)
  - **overlap**: hips lead, chest about 2 frames later, arms 3–5, hands and head last (follow-through)
- **Squash and stretch** on the head and torso on landings, jumps and shock reactions (keep the volume the same).
- **Smears:** on moves faster than about 40° per frame, draw one or two frames with a stretched limb stroke or 2–3 motion lines.
- **Timing on twos:** step the character and its boil to 15 fps (sample the time at `floor(frame/2)`) while camera, captions and the self-drawing chart run at 30 fps. This mix is what reads as "animated" rather than "tweened".
- **Line boil:**
  - shift stroke points 1–2 px by noise, reseeded every 2–3 frames from (reel id, frame/2)
  - SVG feTurbulence/feDisplacementMap is the classic browser trick, but per-point jitter is cheaper and works on canvas
  - keep it subtle: captions sit on top and must stay crisp
- **Background:** off-white paper texture or one flat colour, a ground line and a soft contact shadow, props in rough.js with the same seed per scene.
- **Colour:** black ink plus **one accent colour** for the thing that matters (the chart line, the big number, the highlighted key). A second muted tone at most.
- **Staging:** clear silhouettes (arms away from the body), the figure at 40–60% of frame height, and a camera beat on each new idea.

## STICKMAN REPORT 4: Fit in Marketo

I only read code. Nothing was edited, built or run.

**Bottom line:** a stickman style fits cleanly as a new voiced preset, `stickman`. It reuses the existing beat grammar, so the voice, effects, music, timing, Studio editor and export all work once it is registered in five or six places. There is one real gap: ElevenLabs word timings are fetched and then thrown away, so true lip-sync needs a change to the voice cache.

### 1. How a style works today

**Two separate layers**
- **Channel style** (`shared/styles.ts:10-27`, `STYLES` at :34) is only text: id, label, about, a preview video id, `format`, and the `rules` the AI follows. It has no renderer.
- **Preset** (`shared/presets.ts:28-37`, `PRESETS` at :120) is the field schema plus timing. Its drawing is a function registered with `registerPreset` (`src/render/engine.ts:334-335`) and imported in `src/render/presets/index.ts`.
- **Format** (`shared/formats.ts:9-21`) is a preset plus a starter beat skeleton and hooks.
- **Example** (`shared/examples.ts:12-24`) is a definition turned into `resources/examples/<id>.mp4` by `scripts/make-examples.mjs`. It is the preview on the Styles page.

**What a reel carries**
- `Reel.fields` holds everything. Voiced presets (`VOICED_PRESETS = ['story','narrated','meme']`, presets.ts:74) use a `beats` lines field: `seconds | headline | visual | a | b | say | flags`.
- `storyBeats()` (presets.ts:620-687) parses it into `StoryBeat`: start, dur, visual, a, b, say, voStart, voDur, fx, layout.
- A beat stretches to fit its voice clip (:657). `poll` and `chat` beats get extra time (:661-662).
- Flags are parsed by `beatFlags` (:554-576). Unknown tokens are silently ignored.

**Drawing**
- Every frame is a pure function of (preset, fields, t) on a 1080x1920 Canvas2D (engine.ts:4-9). `drawReel` (:347-371) does: Krypt backdrop → preset draw → end-card fade → disclaimer → vignette and grain (`finish`, :268).
- Preview drives `drawReel` with requestAnimationFrame (`src/components/ReelPlayer.tsx:148-153`).
- Export loops over frames at `t = i/fps` and encodes with mediabunny (`src/render/export.ts:77-90`).
- fps comes from settings: 24, 30 or 60, default 30 (`electron/store.ts:31`, `src/pages/Agents.tsx:138`). Stills use `frame = round(t*30)` (export.ts:172).

**Audio and timing**
- `ensureVoiceover` / `build` (`src/render/voice/index.ts:246-408`) makes one clip per line. It stores `{file, dur}` per clip in the hidden `vo_cache` field (`shared/voice.ts:161-165`), then mixes voice + sfx (`storySfx`, `shared/sfx.ts:138-311`) + music into one track that export attaches (export.ts:53-56).
- The renderer never sees audio, only `voStart` and `voDur`. Word timing is estimated by each word's share of the letters (`wordTimesOf`, `src/render/presets/narrated.ts:24-35`; `wordTimes`, `story.ts:129-141`).
- ElevenLabs is called with timestamps (`electron/eleven.ts:237-239`). But `speakEleven` only uses the timings for `paceOf` (voice/index.ts:202-218) and then drops them.
- No timing data was found for Kokoro.

**Mouth movement today**
- `mouthAt` (`src/render/char3d.ts:624-631`) flaps the mouth by text position. It is used by `drawMascot` (`storyArt.ts:98-114`).
- Loudness-based mouth movement exists only for long-video Kip clips (`src/render/kip.ts:46-68`, async).

**How the AI learns about a style**
- `list_presets` prints `presetSummary` (presets.ts:506-512 → `electron/mcp/tools.ts:1201`).
- `list_formats` prints `formatSummary` (tools.ts:1199).
- Hard-coded lists of names: tools.ts:239 (preset ids), :246 (style ids), :524-525 (save_account styles/tone), :617 (format names).
- `stylesNote()` (tools.ts:907-928) injects the `rules` of every style an account runs. `get_today` names the next style from the mix (tools.ts:109-116, `styleMix`/`nextStyle` styles.ts:352-375).
- `create_reel` takes `style` (tools.ts:1210-1229; `styleTag` :772-784), which saves `Reel.style`.

**Switching on per account**
- Stored in `IgAccount.styles`, set from the Styles page (`src/pages/Styles.tsx:182`, `saveAccount`) or by the AI with `save_account styles` (cleaned by `cleanStyles`, styles.ts:338).

## STICKMAN REPORT 5: Policy and risk

**Bottom line:** No platform bans templated stickman animation or AI voices. Every platform penalises output that looks **mass-produced, unoriginal or reused**, and the penalty usually lands on the whole account (lost recommendations, lost monetisation), not on one video. The risk for us is that every video looks the same, not the stick figure. A narrator voice that sounds realistic triggers an AI label on TikTok and Meta. Our cartoon visuals mostly do not.

### YouTube
- On July 15, 2025 YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". Not allowed for YPP: **"Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"** and **"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production."** Allowed: **"Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different."** If YouTube "cannot clearly tell that you made the content", it can remove monetisation from the whole channel. The policy page does not mention AI voices. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392
- Rene Ritchie called it a "minor update" that clarifies existing rules ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows)).
- Shorts revenue sharing excludes reuploads, "compilations with no original content added" and "artificial or fake views... from automated click or scroll bots". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220

### IP list
1. **A generic stick figure** (circle head, line limbs) is a basic shape and is not protectable on its own. That is the standard idea-vs-expression principle; I found no case law, so this is unverified. **Our own original rig is safe.**
2. **Avoid Alan Becker look-alikes:** the coloured AvA figures (red, blue, green, yellow, orange), The Chosen One, Dark Lord, The Second Coming, and "animator vs. animation" premises such as a cursor or animator fighting the figure. He actively takes down copies ([fan wiki](https://animatorvsanimation.fandom.com/wiki/Alan_Becker_(YouTube_channel))). I found no trademark record either way.
3. **Henry Stickmin** (owned by Innersloth): no bean-shaped head, no tiny-hat stickman, no choice-screen parody.
4. **xkcd** is CC BY-NC 2.5, so no commercial reuse of its drawings ([xkcd.com/license](https://xkcd.com/license.html)). Copying the general look is probably fine; tracing panels is not.
5. **Meme characters** (Trollface, Wojak, Pepe, Rage comics, Stick Nodes community figures): rights are mixed and some are enforced. Do not bake them into the template.
6. **LottieFiles** free files allow commercial use without attribution, but you may not "collect or compile Files from LottieFiles" into a similar service ([licence](https://lottiefiles.com/page/license)). Shipping a pose library inside Marketo built from Lottie downloads could breach that. Files uploaded to LottieFiles under other licences may differ (unverified). **Build or commission our own rig.**
7. **Envato Elements:** a new project needs an active subscription and a registered use ([Envato](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-License-Terms)). That does not fit automated output for many users. Freepik's free tier requires attribution (from memory, **unverified**).
8. **Music and SFX:** TikTok business accounts must use the Commercial Music Library ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article?aid=10005905)). Instagram business accounts are reportedly limited to cleared music (vendor sources only, **unverified**). Safest: royalty-free tracks we own or licence for every platform, or the YouTube Audio Library on YouTube only.
9. **ElevenLabs:** do not clone real people's voices. On YouTube, Meta and TikTok an imitated real person is a disclosure and impersonation issue.

### Guardrails
1. A unique script per video, with a real point (a tip, a number, a step-by-step). Never re-voice the same text.
2. Real screen recordings of the actual tool or app inside the animation in most videos. This is the strongest "made by us" signal, and it rules out "narrating what's already on screen" only if the voice adds steps or opinion.
3. Vary scenes per video: background, props, layout, camera moves and pose order. No fixed scene sequence.
4. Vary the character: colour, accessories and expressions across videos, but keep it clearly our own design.
5. Only a short shared intro/outro (YouTube allows it), or better none. No identical 2-3 second opener; vary the hook.
6. Vary length, pacing and on-screen text style across a batch. Never post near-duplicate videos across accounts on the same platform.
7. Keep the same voice per reel, which the existing rule already requires. Turn on TikTok AIGC and Meta "AI info" when the narrator sounds realistic. YouTube disclosure is not needed for an animated, non-realistic video.
8. Keep posting moderate and spaced (the existing 2h gap). Overposting from one template is listed as a high-risk pattern (air.io, a blog source).
9. Add commentary or opinion ("why this beats X") rather than reading out a list.
10. Own every asset: rig, poses, SFX, music. Keep licence records for Content ID disputes.
11. Mix stickman with other styles on each account so no account is 100% one template. Platforms judge accounts over about 30-day windows.
12. TikTok forbids "automation tools, scripts... to bypass our systems." Posting through a real browser at a human pace lowers the risk but does not remove it. Flag this to users.
13. Check the Instagram/Facebook account status (Professional dashboard / Support home) after the first 10 stickman posts for originality or recommendation flags.
