# Threads research (2026-11-20)

Raw reports from the research swarm, kept as written (model output with sources; check claims before quoting them). The rules the app actually uses are in shared/goals.ts.

**Threads For You ranking: research report (as of 2026-11-20)**

How each source is labelled:
- **[P]** official or primary (Meta Transparency Center, Meta newsroom, creators.instagram.com, or a direct quote from Mosseri or Hayes)
- **[R]** reported by several outlets
- **[B]** a single blog or outlet
- **(pre-2025)** dated before 2025, so possibly outdated

Meta publishes no signal weights for Threads. Every statement below about how strong a signal is comes from Meta's own wording or is labelled as an inference.

### 1. How the For You feed is built

- **The Transparency Center card "Instagram Threads Feed AI system" [P], last updated 2025-03-07.** https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-threads-feed/
  - It works in three steps: gather inventory (part of public Threads content plus posts from accounts you follow), use signals, then rank by "predicted value."
  - The feed blends followed accounts with recommendations.
  - The listed predictions are: like, scroll past, tap profile then another post, tap post then another post, **reply**, click post, click author profile, tap then like, time on the permalink page, and time on the post.
  - The inputs include "likes in the last 15 minutes" and the author's engagement rate (early velocity is a real input). Others are "reply and descendant engagement over the past hour to month" and second-level reply engagement (reply threads count). Time-spent inputs are bucketed by media type.
  - The card does not rank these predictions against each other.
- **Followed accounts are getting more weight.**
  - Nov 2024: Mosseri said Meta was "rebalancing ranking to prioritize content from people you follow" [R] (pre-2025). https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/21/threads-adjusts-its-algorithm-to-show-you-more-content-from-accounts-you-follow
  - 2026-10-14: Connor Hayes said "We recently made a change that could increase the posts you see from those you follow… creators… should see on average more reach to your followers" [P quote via R]. https://www.aol.com/articles/instagram-threads-made-important-change-155547000.html
  - **Implication:** unconnected reach is scarcer for a 0-follower account, so converting viewers into followers matters more than before.

### 2. Signals and how strong each is

- **Replies: the strongest documented lever [P].** "Replies account for almost half of the views on Threads," and replying to others is "about as important as posting your own content."
  - Source: Meta newsroom, 2024-10-21, updated 2025-06-26 (https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/find-your-community-with-new-threads-educational-insights/), and creators.instagram.com/threads (data from H1 2024).
  - "Likelihood to reply" is a ranked prediction, and reply depth feeds permalink time.
  - Limit: Mosseri said "Not all comments or replies are good" (Oct 2024, engagement-bait crackdown) [R] (pre-2025). https://www.engadget.com/social-media/meta-is-working-to-fix-threads-engagement-bait-problem-173135011.html
- **Likes, profile taps and follow-through [P].** Likes (including recent velocity), profile taps and "tap profile then another post" are listed predictions. The card has no separate "follow from post" prediction, but the profile-tap chains are the closest one.
- **Dwell [P].** Time on the post and time on the permalink are both predictions. "Scroll past" is a negative prediction.
- **Reposts and quotes.** They are not named in the card. Quotes create new posts and conversations. Their weight is unknown [inference].
- **Hide, "not interested", mute and report [P].** Hide "helps to limit similar content." Report means "show less similar." Swipe-left to mark "not interested" was tested in 2024 [R] (pre-2025).
- **Instagram activity.** The March 2025 card does not spell out Instagram signals. Buffer (2026-01-26) reads Instagram profile views as a signal [B], so the sources conflict.
  - Meta says more than a third of daily users with connections mostly follow different accounts on Threads than on Instagram [P, 2025-07-01]. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/07/introducing-messaging-highlighted-perspectives-threads/
  - Eligible creator Threads posts can be recommended on Instagram and Facebook [P, 2025-03]. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/03/new-threads-features/
  - Sign-up without Instagram (via Facebook, email or phone) varies by region [R].
  - **Bottom line:** Instagram followers help only through the follow prompts. A faceless Threads-only account starts cold.

### 3. Topics, communities, feeds and trending

- **Tags: one per post, by design [P/R] (pre-2025, still current).** Mosseri said the goal was "less on engagement hacking."
- **Tags help [P].** Meta says "posts with tagged topics generally get more views" (internal data). Profiles can list up to 10 topics, and the composer suggests trending topics. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/03/new-threads-features/
- **Communities [P].**
  - Launched 2025-10-02 with 100+ topics, later 200+. Meta creates them and users can't. You reach them through blue topic tags. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/introducing-threads-communities-find-your-people
  - 2026-06-16: communities left beta and Meta added a Community Hub, champions, local communities and Live Chats. https://about.fb.com/br/news/2026/06/novos-recursos-para-celebrar-500-milhoes-de-usuarios-mensais-no-threads/
  - **Implication:** tagging a post with an existing community's tag is the cheapest topical distribution.
- **Custom feeds [P/R].** Keyword or topic feeds launched Nov 2024, became public and shareable in Feb 2025, and can be set as the default feed. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/share-custom-feeds-on-threads/
- **Trending Now [P].** Machine-learned list of topics people are engaging with now. Launched 2024-03 (pre-2025). https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/threads-trending-now
- **Highlighter [P].** Marks "unique perspectives" in Trending. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/07/introducing-messaging-highlighted-perspectives-threads/
- **Gems [R], test started 2026-10-08, US only.** An automated daily badge for public posts and **direct** replies (not replies to replies), chosen for perspective, discussion and originality. Cross-posts and "recycled" content are ineligible. No ranking effect has been confirmed. https://socialmediatoday.com/news/threads-recognizes-top-posts-with-gems/832577

### 4. Formats

- **Links: no intentional penalty [P].**
  - Nov 2024: Mosseri said links aren't downranked, but "people don't like and comment on links much" (pre-2025).
  - June 2025: "We've been working on making sure links are ranked properly… Links have been working much better for more than a month."
  - Click-through is still low [R]. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-says-link-posts-ranked-properly-threads-reach/750126/ ; https://www.engadget.com/social-media/threads-users-still-barely-click-links-170139103.html
- **Media plus text [P].** "Video, photo, and carousel posts that include text have performed significantly better" than media without text. Meta advises mixing text-only posts with media posts, and says humor gets more views. https://creators.instagram.com/threads
- **Text attachments [P].** Up to 10,000 characters, text-only posts, no polls, launched 2025-09-04. They are not federated or indexed by search engines [R]. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/attach-text-threads-posts-share-longer-perspectives/
- **Polls, GIFs and video length.**
  - Polls and GIFs both exist, with no ranking data.
  - Video length of up to 5 minutes is claimed by vendor blogs [B].
  - The feed crops video to 4:5 per Meta's ad specs; whether this applies to organic posts is unconfirmed [B].

### 5. How replies rank under posts

- No primary source documents reply-sort order on Threads.
- What exists:
  - The card's reply and descendant-engagement signals.
  - Author tools: follower-only replies (2025-03), plus reply approvals and filters (2025-10-30) [P].
  - Gems for direct replies.
- **Inference:** replies that draw their own replies and likes rise, and direct replies to the original post are the recognized unit.

### 6. Recommended accounts for new users

- No Threads-specific primary source.
- On sign-up you are prompted to follow your Instagram follows [R].
- With "for you" leaning toward followed accounts (Oct 2026), a new account's main way to get discovered is replying on bigger posts and in community tags.

- Post "at least 2-5 times per week." Higher frequency is "linked to higher impressions per post."
- Weekends may raise engagement. Meta names no specific hours.

### 9. Reach penalties

- **Engagement bait, clickbait, contests and giveaways** "can limit distribution." "Original content gets the best reach." Cross-posting from Instagram or Facebook is OK when starting out, but "Threads-specific text content" is stronger [P]. https://creators.instagram.com/threads
- **Recommendation Guidelines** are stricter than Community Guidelines. Ineligible content stays up but is not recommended, and repeat offenders can lose recommendation eligibility for a period [P, Instagram help, applied across Instagram and Threads]. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators
- **Spam networks:** Meta cut reach for spam networks in 2025 [R, Facebook-specific]. https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/24/facebook-cracks-down-on-spammy-content-by-cutting-reach-and-monetization
- **Rate limits:** Threads has had rate limits since 2023 [R] (pre-2025).
- **AI labels:** "AI info" labels apply on Threads [R].

### 10. Conversation or broadcast?

Threads favors conversation:
- Replies drive almost half of all views [P].
- Reply prediction and reply depth are ranking inputs [P].
- Hayes: "A conversation app needs to work wherever you are" [P quote, 2026-05].
- Highlighter and Gems reward perspective and discussion.

Broadcast posts still reach followers, especially after the Oct 2026 shift.

### 11. Myths contradicted by primary sources

1. **"Links are shadow-penalized."** Mosseri says no; link posts have been ranked "properly" since mid-2025. They just get fewer likes and comments.
2. **"Use many hashtags."** Only one tag per post is allowed, and tagged posts get more views.
3. **"Only your own posts matter."** Replies are about half of all views.
4. **"Posting often hurts reach."** Meta links higher frequency to more impressions per post.
5. **"Political posts are never recommended."** That stopped in Jan 2025.
6. **"Ask-a-question bait boosts you."** Meta says engagement bait limits distribution. It recommends an opinion plus a question or personal story instead.
7. **"For You is mostly strangers now."** Since Nov 2024 and Oct 2026, followed accounts have been prioritized.
8. **"There's a magic posting hour."** Meta only says weekends may help.

### Rules for an AI agent posting and replying on Threads for a small faceless account

1. **Reply first, post second.** Each session, spend most effort on thoughtful **direct** replies to fresh posts (under 1 hour old) in your niche: PC tweaks, Windows tools, prediction markets, AI trading. Each reply should add a specific fact or opinion, never "great post." Replies are about half of all views and qualify for Gems.
2. **Write like a person.** Keep replies short, lowercase-loose and varied. Never post templated or repeated text. Spam-network detection and "not all replies are good" make sameness the biggest risk. Respect the hub's per-thread reply cap.
3. **One topic tag on every post,** matching an existing Meta community or Trending topic where it fits (for example, a Windows or PC community, or AI). Don't stuff tags into the text.
4. **Always pair media with text.** Every screen-demo video or reel needs a hook line or opinion, and ideally a question. Alternate media posts with text-only posts. Keep videos short and readable in a 4:5 crop.
5. **Make it Threads-native.** Write a fresh caption for Threads rather than an auto cross-post, and don't recycle viral text. Original content gets the best reach, and cross-posts can't win Gems. Label realistic AI voice or video where Meta requires an AI disclosure.
6. **Links are OK, but sparing.** Put the link in a self-reply or at the end of a text attachment, and keep the main post value-first. Expect low clicks rather than a penalty.
7. **No engagement bait.** No "comment YES," giveaways or rage-bait. For Polymarket bots, avoid "guaranteed profit" claims and avoid political rage-bait around election markets.
8. **Answer every reply on your own posts.** Do it quickly within the first hour: reply depth and early likes ("likes in last 15 min") feed ranking. Use reply approvals or filters only against spam.
9. **Cadence: 1 post a day,** with at least 2-5 a week per Meta's guidance, spaced out, plus daily replies. Lean in on weekends. Check Insights rather than assuming a magic hour.
10. **Grow followers, not just views.** The Oct 2026 change favors followed accounts. Keep the profile clear (bio, up to 10 topics, pinned demo) so profile taps turn into follows. Check Account status for recommendation eligibility before any volume increase, and back off at any warning or rate limit.

# Threads follower growth for a faceless, AI-run account (as of 2026-11-20)

**Evidence labels:** [META] means Meta or Mosseri said it. [DATA] is a vendor dataset with a stated method (Buffer, Metricool). [VENDOR] is a vendor blog with a loose or unclear method. [SELF] is a single self-reported account. [EST] is my own estimate.

**Overall caveat:** no controlled study links any Threads tactic to follower gain. Every large dataset measures engagement or views, and none measures follows. Treat the ranking below as reasoned, not proven.

## Platform facts that matter
- **The feed favours accounts you follow.** Since Nov 2024, Mosseri has rebalanced ranking toward followed accounts, so reaching non-followers is harder than in year one [META].
- **Instagram activity feeds the ranking.** The For You feed predicts likes, replies, reposts and follows. It also uses some Instagram signals, such as visits to the author's Instagram profile [META, via Sendible's summary of Meta's system card].
- **Replies carry about half of all views.** Meta (Oct 2024, updated Jun 2025) said "replies account for almost half of the views on Threads". It also said posts that drive conversation are more likely to be recommended, that funny content gets more views, and that top creators post original content made for Threads [META].
- **Communities launched in Oct 2025 and left beta in Jun 2026.** Threads now has 500M monthly users [META].
- **Users can steer their own feeds.** "Dear Algo" (Feb 2026) and "Your Algo" (Jun 2026) let users shape what they see.
- **Ads reach every user since Jan 2026,** per secondary reports.

## What kills reach
- **Engagement bait.** Mosseri said in Oct 2024 that Meta is "working to get it under control". Secondhand reports add that it is down-ranking "nice post" and emoji-only replies.
- **Rage bait.** Meta announced plans to reduce it.
- **Repurposed cross-platform posts.** Buffer's tester and Sendible both saw them underperform, and Meta says top creators post original content. Facebook's July 2025 unoriginal-content rules cut reach for repeat reposters; I found no proof Threads applies them.
- **Links.** Mosseri said in June 2025 that links are not penalized but people engage with them less. Link posts sit at the bottom of Buffer's medians, and Engadget reports little Threads referral traffic. Put links in the bio or a later reply.
- **Repeated text and AI-sounding replies.** I found no Threads-specific crackdown. X removed 42,000 chatbot-reply accounts in July 2026, and Meta's automated moderation is banning aggressively; more than 60k people have petitioned over wrongful bans.
- **Automation itself.** Instagram and Threads terms bar automated activity without permission, so an AI-run browser account is inherently grey. Risk scales with volume, identical timing and identical text.
- **Profit or hype claims.** For Polymarket content these are an integrity risk as well as a brand risk.

## Realistic numbers
**Measured anchors:**
- **Buffer 30-day test (Sep 2024):** 267 to 290 followers on 13,793 views, about **1.7 follows per 1,000 views**.
- **Buffer growth plan (Oct 2025):** 366 to 431 in about a month.
- **Sendible brand account:** about **5k followers over 14 months** on at least 460k reported views. That works out to roughly 4–10 follows per 1,000 views, with some quarters unreported.
- **Metricool (2025 data, 1M accounts):** Threads averages about 1,536 impressions and 25 interactions per post at 6.5 posts a week.

**Estimate for a new faceless niche account with no IG audience, posting daily and replying a lot [EST]:**

| Day | Followers |
|---|---|
| 30 | 30–200 |
| 90 | 200–1,000 |
| 180 | 600–3,000 |

- **Follows per 1,000 views:** 1–5 is normal. Clear niche value plus a strong profile (bio says what you get, pinned post) pushes toward 5–10.
- **Outliers:** one viral post (100k–1M views) can add 500–3,000 followers in days. An unverified Notion "starter kit" claims 10k in under two months, about 1,800 of them in week 1.

## "Mass growth" playbook for Threads

**Before the first post**
- **Profile:** a clear niche in the bio ("free Windows tools + AI tools, no paid junk"), one link only, and a branded avatar since the account is faceless.
- **Link to the IG account** so the Threads badge and Story sharing work.
- **Warm-up (days 1–5):** browse, like and follow 10–20 niche accounts a day, plus Krypt's accounts per the standing rule. Reply lightly, 5–10 a day.
- **Seed the first post** around day 3–5.
- **Line up 3–5 pinned or evergreen posts:** a "best free Windows tools" list with a screenshot, and a short screen clip of the tweaker.

**Daily (soft caps the user can override)**
- **Posts:** 2–4, spaced at least 2 hours apart. Weekday windows of about 6–11am and 7–9pm local are good starting points to test (Buffer, Teract).
  - About 60% conversation posts: a question or mild opinion in under 200 characters.
  - About 30% media: a screenshot or 10–20 s screen clip with a one-line caption.
  - About 10% product mentions, with any link in a reply, not the post.
- **Polymarket posts:** "AI does the trading for you, easy." No profit numbers and no screenshots of P&L.
- **Replies to others:** 25–40 a day, at most 2 per thread (hub cap).
  - **Who:** a target list of 30–60 accounts at 20k–500k followers who post daily in PC gaming and performance, Windows, AI tools and agents, and Polymarket or prediction markets. Add whoever trends in the matching Community.
  - **Speed:** first 10–20 replies, ideally within 15–30 minutes of their post. A late reply on a 2-hour-old big post is mostly wasted.
  - **Style:** about 5 words, lowercase okay, specific to the post, never "great post", no emoji-only replies, no link drops, and a varied opener every time. Examples: "ran this on my old laptop lol", "polymarket fees eat that edge tho".
- **Own replies:** answer every real commenter within 1–2 hours of posting (the +42% lift).
- **Tags:** one specific topic tag or Community per post, rotated across 3–5 niche tags.

**Weekly**
- **Insights:** pull 7-day views, follows, and follows per 1,000 views per post type and per reply target.
- **Repost** the best post of the week to the IG Story.
- **Trends:** do one trend-jacking post the day news drops (Windows update, new AI model, Polymarket headline).
- **Target list:** replace the 20% of reply targets that brought no profile visits or follows.
- **Duplicates:** run a check for duplicate or near-duplicate text across the week.

**What to measure**
- Follows per 1,000 views, by format and by "reply on X" versus own post.
- Replies per post, and reply rate on your own posts.
- Profile visits from replies.
- Share of posts above the account median.
- Reach to non-followers.

**When to drop or double a format**
- **Judge** after at least 8 posts of a format over at least 2 weeks.
- **Double it** when views or follows per 1,000 views are at least 1.5x the account median.
- **Drop it** below 0.5x.
- **Reply targets:** drop any account that gives 0 profile visits after 10 replies.
- **Warning signs:** if per-post views fall below 50% of the 14-day median for 5 days, or the account sees an action-block warning, cut replies by half and posting to 1–2 a day for 3–4 days. Check Account Status before resuming.

## Tactics ranked by expected customers

1. **Answering people who ask for a tool, found through Threads search.** Evidence: none specific to Threads.
   - Secondary sources say Mosseri holds that replies collectively count as much as posts. [P/S] https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-grow-on-threads/
   - This is ranked first because the intent is already there: someone asking "any free FPS booster?" is close to downloading.
   - Write a real, specific answer, then add the link only when they explicitly asked for a tool. Otherwise point to "link's on my profile."
   - Repeating identical link replies across threads is exactly the "repetitive content" spam signal (see Rules).

2. **Native demo videos and screen recordings in the post, link in your own first reply.**
   - Video ranks second in Buffer's data. [V]
   - Link in a reply vs in the post: the only Threads test is one creator's 24-hour experiment claiming about 11x reach. [S, very weak] https://drmercedessamudio.substack.com/p/what-a-24-hour-threads-experiment
   - LinkedIn tests point the same way. Mosseri's 2025 statement argues against a big penalty, so this is worth testing, not assuming.
   - Fit: Windows tools are visual (FPS counter, freed RAM, crosshair overlay), so recordings sell them well.

3. **Before/after and comparisons vs paid tools.**
   - No Threads data. Pictures lead Buffer's format data, so before/after screenshot carousels fit.
   - Paid-rival prices must be sourced and current. Meta's "Product misrepresentation" rule applies.

4. **Profile as a landing page: 5 bio links plus a pinned post.**
   - The 5 links are [O]. The pinned-post limit appears to be one post, from a non-official source.
   - Every viewer who taps the profile sees this, and it costs nothing in reach.
   - Suggested: bio link 1 = krypt.cc tools page (UTM tagged). Bio link 2 = the bots page. Pinned post = a 20-30s demo with "what it does / Windows 10-11 / link in bio."

5. **Link-attachment posts (preview card).**
   - Allowed, and Meta says they're ranked "properly" now. But the click data [P] and engagement data [V] are poor.
   - Use for launches and updates, roughly 1 in 4 posts.

6. **Build-in-public posts (changelogs, "added X because you asked").** No data at all. These build follows and trust more than they drive direct downloads.

7. **DMs.** Useful only after someone asks. Reply in their thread or DM once. A link sent to a non-follower sits in Requests with links disabled, so cold DMs with links don't work. Never mass-DM.

8. **Topic tags and Communities.**
   - One tag per post. Communities launched October 2025 [O], had 200+ topics by December 2025, and left beta on 2026-06-16 (500M MAU). https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/introducing-threads-communities/ and https://about.fb.com/news/2026/06/meta-launching-new-features-500-million-monthly-threads-users/
   - The listed communities are mostly sports and entertainment. Whether there are PC-gaming, tech or AI communities is **unverified**; check in the app.
   - Low cost, so tag every post (e.g. "PC Gaming", "Windows").

9. **Cross-posting from Instagram.**
   - It covers feed images only, not Reels (2024 reports; no 2025-2026 change found). https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/22/how-to-cross-post-from-instagram-and-facebook-to-threads
   - Low value. Native Threads posts with a native first reply are better.
   - Threads posts can be shared to Instagram Stories (official, February 2026).

10. **Keyword-comment auto-DM ("comment FPS and I'll DM it").** No supported tool for Threads and no DM API. Doing it through browser automation risks spam enforcement, and links to non-followers land in Requests. **Skip it.**

11. **Ads (not needed; for reference).**

    - Online gambling covers "anything of monetary value" in both entry and prize, needs authorization, and is 18+ only. https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/gambling-games
    - Meta has **no prediction-market-specific ad policy** (secondary source, October 2026). Assume promoting Polymarket bots in ads falls under the gambling or financial rules.

12. **Paid partnership label.**
    - Threads has it: the creator tags the brand, and the brand can't remove the label. Help page (date unclear): https://help.instagram.com/2467529163409127

    - If Krypt pays creators (creator program) to post about the tools, they should use the label.

## Rules that matter (organic posts)
- **Spam** (Meta Community Standards, which cover Threads; NY AG filing 2025): https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/
  - Posting or engaging "at very high frequencies," whether manual or automated.
  - Repetitive content is a signal that can restrict even low-frequency accounts.
  - Misleading links and deceptive redirects are banned.
  - Buying or exchanging engagement, and "offering anything of monetary value in exchange for engagement," are banned.
- **Prohibited Commercial Practices** (changelog dated 2026-10-29; Meta is folding scam rules into one set for organic posts and ads). https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/prohibited-commercial-practices/ Banned:
  - investment offers that are "guaranteed or risk-free"
  - offers claiming "quick returns (such as under 24 hours)"
  - offers "claiming or referencing successful past performance or returns"
  - offers of "unrealistic financial reward for unclear or minimal effort"
  - gambling offers "with a guarantee of winning"
- **This directly affects the bot posts.** No P&L screenshots, no win-rate or return claims, no "made $X." The current house line "AI trades for you, easy" sits close to "minimal effort" financial reward. Pair it with "you set the limits, markets carry risk, losses happen" and never imply income.
- **Organic gambling posts:** I could not verify whether Meta age-gates or limits organic posts that promote real-money gambling. Treat it as unverified.
- **API caps** for reference: 250 posts and 1,000 replies per rolling 24 hours. https://developers.facebook.com/docs/threads/overview

## Measuring
- **UTM on every link.** Use `utm_source=threads&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<tool>&utm_content=<bio|pinned|reply|post-id>`. Placement goes in `utm_content`, so bio, pinned, first-reply and post links can be compared.
- Threads traffic can land as "referral" rather than organic social in analytics, so check both.
- Count downloads per UTM on krypt.cc. That is the real metric, since in-app link clicks are gone.
- If an API token is ever used, pull the API `clicks` per URL. That is user-level only, with no per-post breakdown.
- Watch per-post views, replies and reposts, plus profile views from the API. Treat follows-per-post as **unverified** in the current in-app insights.

## Push product playbook (Threads)

**Funnel:** search intent and native demo posts, then the profile (5 bio links and the pinned demo), then a krypt.cc page with UTM, then the download. The bots get their own page, with risk text and no performance claims.

**Daily** (defaults to suggest; the user's own limits and post gaps always win):
- **2-3 native posts.** One demo video (10-30s screen recording), one before/after image or carousel, one short text post (tip or question).
  - One topic tag each.
  - Link only in your own first reply, on at most 1-2 of them.
  - About 1 in 4 posts can use a link-attachment card instead, for comparison.
- **Search-driven replies.** Search phrases like "fps boost", "debloat windows", "free crosshair", "macro software", "video editor free", "polymarket bot".
  - Answer people asking in the last 24-48h with a specific, human-sounding reply (~5-15 words, no LLM tone).
  - Link only on an explicit ask, never the same wording twice, max 2 replies per thread.
- **Your own comments.** Reply to every comment on your posts in the first hour; Buffer reports +42% engagement from 128k posts [V]. Move to DM only when the person asks.
- **Bot posts.** At most 1 in 5 posts, framed as "an AI agent trades within limits you set."
  - Never profit claims, P&L, win rates, "free to use" or "open source."
  - Add a risk line and say it's 18+ only.

**Weekly:**
- Refresh the pinned demo.
- Post one "vs paid tool" comparison with sourced prices.
- Post one build-in-public changelog.
- Post one long-form text attachment (up to 10,000 characters) as a how-to guide.
- Review downloads by `utm_content`. Keep the best placement, retest the losing one.
- Check bio links still resolve, so nothing counts as a "misleading link."

**Measure:** downloads per 1,000 views by format and by link placement; profile views; follower growth; reply-to-download conversion for search replies (tag those links `utm_content=search-reply`).

**Main risks for an AI agent:**
1. Repetitive link replies or high-volume replying trip the spam rules and lead to restrictions. Vary text, cap volume, and post links only on request.
2. Financial-claim violations on the bot posts (past returns, quick or effortless money).
3. LLM-sounding replies get reported as bots.
4. Browser automation for DMs or auto-DM flows: no API exists, and links are blocked in requests.
5. Unsigned Windows downloads trigger SmartScreen. That's no Meta rule, but if the landing page doesn't match the post it reads as a "misleading link." Make the landing page match the post exactly.
6. Comparison claims about rival tools must be accurate and current.

**Evidence gaps:** no controlled tests of link vs no link, or link in post vs link in reply; no data on Threads-to-download conversion for software; the size of the DM request cap; current in-app follows-per-post; whether tech or PC communities exist; whether organic gambling posts are age-gated.

## 7. Hard rules for an AI agent running Threads in a browser

1. **Instagram first.** Before every session, check Instagram and Threads Account Status. If either shows a restriction or "not recommended", skip growth actions and post or read only.
2. **Stop on any block.** On "Try again later" or "We limit how often…", stop that action type for 48 hours, never retry, and cut that action's limit by half for a week.
3. **No repeated text.** Every reply must be unique and specific to the post, short and loosely typed. Never paste templates, and never send the same line twice in 24 hours.
4. **No engagement swaps.** No follow trains, "follow for follow", buying engagement or reply-pods: these are explicitly banned.
5. **Links sparingly.** None in week 1, none in DM requests (they are blocked there anyway), at most 1 in 5 posts afterwards, and never a link that misleads about where it goes.
6. **Human pacing.** Randomised 20–120-second gaps, sessions of 15–40 minutes, real scrolling and reading between actions, and nothing bursty (for example, at most 10 follows in any hour).
7. **One device, one identity.** Use the same Chrome profile, IP and proxy for the paired Instagram and Threads accounts. Never run several Threads profiles from one fingerprint, since that is "network of accounts" risk.
8. **Use the API for posting when possible.** Use the official Threads API (own app, user as tester) for scheduled posts. Keep the browser for reading, likes, follows and replies.
9. **Separate Instagram and Threads.** Don't run heavy phases on both platforms the same day, and don't cross-post identical captions to both within minutes.
10. **Log every action with timestamps** so a block can be traced to what caused it and the user can see it in Export logs.

**Patterns that repeat across the winners**

1. **Text comes first and is short.** One line with one concrete fact or number. "BREAKING: X now 70%" beats any long caption.
2. **Links go in a follow-up, not the opening post.** The link sits in a self-reply (Rowan, Polymarket) or in the bio (chatgptricks, Kalshi). Sendible says links and images cut its reach. Other sources say there is no link penalty, so treat that as one account's experience.
3. **Numbered self-reply chains.** Marc Lou, Lennox Saint and Rowan Cheung all run 1/, 2/, 3/ threads. Each part is a separate chance to be shown.
4. **Faceless AI accounts run on memes.** chatgptricks (the biggest in this set) posts relatable one-liners and never a hard sell, and keeps the product in the bio.
5. **Replies are half the game.** Meta says half of Threads views come from replies. An Indie Hackers founder who says he got his first 100 paying users from Threads spent more time in others' comments than on his own posts.
6. **Volume in bursts.** Polymarket and The Rundown post 6–9 times a day around news. Both are brands, not people.
7. **Questions and "roll call" posts pull comments.** @hey.rachael's roll call got 48 comments against 7 likes or fewer on her other posts.
8. **Cross-posting from Instagram and Reels is normal.** Kalshi and Polymarket Sports send the bio link to Instagram, and Buffer's piece on @rita_codes says cross-posted videos did well for her. Sendible found that reposting from X or Bluesky flopped.
9. **Consistency is the bottleneck.** Sendible's engagement fell to about 650 a month when daily community work stopped. The founder of a scheduler sold on Threads says growth stalled when he stopped posting by hand.
10. **Product-led growth on Threads is slow.** BlackTwist (a Threads scheduler) reached about $1.5k MRR in a year: "not explosive, but steady", driven by an affiliate program and creators naming it in "tools I use" posts.

**What stalled or gets penalized**

- **Big brand accounts that go quiet or only cross-post:** @windows has 589K followers and its newest visible post is from May 2024. @kalshi gets 7–13 likes per post on 15K followers. Polymarket Sports gets 1–5 likes. Follower count does not buy reach.
- **Engagement bait:** Mosseri admitted Threads has an engagement-bait problem and said a fix was coming. Meta also said it would cut down rage-bait. Practitioner guides say "comment YES" posts lose reach (not Meta-confirmed). Nvidia's comment-to-enter giveaway is the kind of post this targets.
- **Crypto and trading scam reply spam:** Engadget tied the MrBeast reply spam on Threads to more than 10,000 crypto-casino sites. The accounts were hacked or brand new and hid site names in blurry screenshots. Meta is removing some of them. Any trading-bot reply with screenshots plus prize or profit talk looks exactly like this.
- **Fake P&L and revenue:** The maker scene now checks numbers. Marc Lou's hackathon requires verified revenue (TrustMRR), and his #1 entry was disqualified. Polymarket-bot profit claims on Telegram are unverified marketing. A DEV piece argues that leading with revenue numbers turns building in public into a performance.
- **Follow trains:** I found no Threads-specific source on these. Meta's guidelines, which Threads uses, ban artificially collecting followers or likes. Treat follow trains as a risk.
- **AI reply spam:** I found no Meta statement specific to Threads. The scam reply networks above get mass takedowns, and Meta's 2026 anti-scam push uses AI detection. Generic, LLM-sounding replies are the obvious next target (my inference).
